# Naming, Versioning, and Code Evolution

**Status:** Index. Sourced 2026-08-03. Annotations are notes and observations, not substrate policy — see `00-index.md` §"How to read this".

## Why this theme

`formalism/paper/arbor-core.tex`'s headline theorem is called **no silent breakage**, and this is the literature that theorem is a claim about. It splits into three parts, each with a lesson for arbor:

1. **Semver and breaking changes.** A decade of empirical work says version numbers do not carry the information developers need. But the sharpest paper in the set (Ochoa et al.) also says most breaking changes never *reach* clients — which means **arbor's motivation must rest on certainty and verification cost, not on breakage frequency.** That is a real constraint on how the theorem gets pitched.
2. **Structured diff and merge.** The whole tradition is the file world trying to recover, by heuristic, the structural information a structured store has by construction. Read the aspiration/evaluation pairs (MolhadoRef 2007 with Ellis et al. 2023) rather than the aspirations alone: operation-based refactoring-aware version control underdelivers when the operation log is *reconstructed* rather than authored. **That is the argument for arbor recording intent at authoring time**, which is what p10/p11's mint marks are.
3. **CRDTs and replicated trees.** arbor's hierarchical dotted namespace, under concurrent agentic writers, *is* a replicated tree with moves and renames. Kleppmann et al. demonstrate actual bugs in Google Drive and Dropbox under concurrent moves. Ignoring this risks a namespace layer that silently loses bindings — and "silently loses bindings" is the exact failure arbor is named after preventing.

## Semantic versioning as an object

- **Tom Preston-Werner — "Semantic Versioning 2.0.0."** [semver.org](https://semver.org/). `[not peer-reviewed — spec]` Normative but informal: it *requires* a "precise and comprehensive" public API declaration and leaves its identity to the author. **arbor's position is that a hash discharges that obligation.** Note also that semver is about version-number conventions and is unrelated to "semantic diff" — do not conflate them.
- **Patrick Lam, Jens Dietrich, David J. Pearce (Onward! 2020) — "Putting the Semantics into Semantic Versioning."** DOI [10.1145/3426428.3426922](https://doi.org/10.1145/3426428.3426922); arXiv:2008.07069. `[verified]` **[Priority read.]** Argues for "semantic version calculators" that decide whether an upgrade is likely safe, using behavioural contracts rather than type signatures alone. **The closest existing framing to arbor's headline theorem** — it wants exactly the object arbor could supply, and documents why type-shape checking is insufficient. This is the venue and vocabulary to pitch no-silent-breakage into.
- **Elm's automatic semantic versioning** (`elm bump` / `elm diff`). [elm/compiler docs](https://github.com/elm/compiler/blob/master/docs/elm.json/package.md); community [critique thread](https://discourse.elm-lang.org/t/semver-enforcement/9263). `[not peer-reviewed — artifact]` The compiler computes the bump by diffing the published API. Important negative datapoint: the Elm community itself notes this is type-shape enforcement only, not behavioural — the same boundary eval-stability sits on.
- **`cargo-semver-checks`** (Predrag Gruevski et al.). [Repo](https://github.com/obi1kenobi/cargo-semver-checks); [Rust project goal](https://rust-lang.github.io/rust-project-goals/2025h1/cargo-semver-checks.html). `[not peer-reviewed — artifact]` **`rust-semverver` is the deprecated predecessor** (unmaintained since ~2023, depended on compiler internals) — cite this instead.

## The empirical breaking-change line

- **Steven Raemaekers, Arie van Deursen, Joost Visser (SCAM 2014) — "Semantic Versioning versus Breaking Changes: A Study of the Maven Repository."** pp. 215–224. DOI [10.1109/SCAM.2014.30](https://doi.org/10.1109/SCAM.2014.30). `[verified]` ~1/3 of releases introduce a breaking change, at the *same rate* for minor and major: "version numbers do not provide developers with information about stability of interfaces." Journal extension in *JSS* 129:140–158, 2017 `[verified — secondary]`.
- **Lina Ochoa, Thomas Degueule, Jean-Rémy Falleri, Jurgen Vinju (EMSE 2022) — "Breaking Bad? Semantic Versioning and Impact of Breaking Changes in Maven Central."** 27(3):61. DOI [10.1007/s10664-021-10052-y](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10664-021-10052-y); arXiv:2110.07889. `[verified]` **[Priority read — as the sharpest critique of arbor's motivation.]** Many breaking changes exist but relatively few actually reach clients. So arbor's pitch has to be built on *certainty and verification cost*, not breakage frequency.
- **Dhanushka Jayasuriya, Valerio Terragni, Jens Dietrich, Samuel Ou, Kelly Blincoe (ISSTA 2023) — "Understanding Breaking Changes in the Wild."** DOI [10.1145/3597926.3598147](https://doi.org/10.1145/3597926.3598147). Extended: "An extended study of syntactic breaking changes in the wild," *EMSE* 30(2):42, 2025, DOI [10.1007/s10664-024-10563-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10664-024-10563-4). `[verified]` **First author is Jayasuriya, not Dietrich.** The source/binary/behavioural taxonomy — **arbor eliminates the source and link classes by construction; behavioural is where eval-stability has to work**, and where it is both novel and hardest to state.
- **Jens Dietrich, David J. Pearce, Jacob Stringer, Amjed Tahir, Kelly Blincoe (MSR 2019) — "Dependency Versioning in the Wild."** pp. 349–359. DOI [10.1109/MSR.2019.00061](https://doi.org/10.1109/MSR.2019.00061). `[verified]` The empirical distribution of pin-vs-float — direct evidence for the pin/follow/explicit axis in `../design/04-naming-layer.md` §"Update strategies" and p11.
- **Alexandre Decan & Tom Mens (IEEE TSE 2021) — "What Do Package Dependencies Tell Us About Semantic Versioning?"** 47(6):1226–1240. DOI [10.1109/TSE.2019.2918315](https://doi.org/10.1109/TSE.2019.2918315). `[verified]` Cross-ecosystem evidence that dependency *constraints* encode maintainers' distrust of semver — the failure mode a separate namespace layer removes.
- **Laerte Xavier, Aline Brito, Andre Hora, Marco Tulio Valente (SANER 2017) — "Historical and Impact Analysis of API Breaking Changes: A Large-Scale Study."** pp. 138–147. [PDF](https://homepages.dcc.ufmg.br/~mtov/pub/2017-saner-breaking-apis.pdf). `[verified — DOI unconfirmed]` Breaking-change frequency *increases over time*, and high-breakage libraries are larger, more popular, more active.
- **Aline Brito, Laerte Xavier, Andre Hora, Marco Tulio Valente (SANER 2018) — "APIDiff: Detecting API Breaking Changes."** [IEEE](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/8330249/); [tool](https://github.com/aserg-ufmg/apidiff). `[verified]` **SANER 2018, not SBES 2018.** It matches API elements across versions by a *static similarity heuristic* — precisely the guesswork p10/p11's minted marks replace with a recorded intent signal.
- **Corentin Latappy, Thomas Degueule, Jean-Rémy Falleri, Romain Robbes, Lina Ochoa (ICSME 2025) — "Roseau: Fast, Accurate, Source-based API Breaking Change Analysis in Java."** arXiv:[2507.17369](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.17369). `[verified]` Commit-level detection; F1 = 0.99 vs JApiCmp's 0.86; 6,839 Guava commits from days to minutes. The performance and granularity bar content addressing gets for free.

## Operation-based and refactoring-aware version control

- **Ernst Lippe & Norbert van Oosterom (SDE 5, 1992) — "Operation-Based Merging."** pp. 78–87. DOI [10.1145/142868.143753](https://doi.org/10.1145/142868.143753). `[verified]` Merge by composing *recorded operations* rather than reconstructing intent from state diffs. The ancestor of both CRDTs and arbor's edit calculus.
- **Danny Dig, Kashif Manzoor, Ralph E. Johnson, Tien N. Nguyen (ICSE 2007) — "Refactoring-Aware Configuration Management for Object-Oriented Programs"** (MolhadoRef). pp. 427–436. DOI [10.1109/ICSE.2007.71](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICSE.2007.71). `[verified]` **Note:** the frequently-surfaced DOI `10.1145/1218776.1218797` is a *different, shorter* SIGSOFT SEN item — cite the ICSE DOI.
- **Max Ellis, Sarah Nadi, Danny Dig (IEEE TSE 2023) — "Operation-Based Refactoring-Aware Merging: An Empirical Evaluation."** 49(4):2698–2721. DOI [10.1109/TSE.2022.3228851](https://doi.org/10.1109/TSE.2022.3228851); arXiv:2112.10370. `[verified]` **Read as a pair with MolhadoRef** — the aspiration and its honest sixteen-years-later evaluation. Together they show operation-based refactoring-aware VC underdelivers when the operation log is *reconstructed* rather than authored. **That is the argument for arbor recording intent at authoring time.**
- **Bo Shen, Wei Zhang, Haiyan Zhao, Guangtai Liang, Zhi Jin, Qianxiang Wang (OOPSLA 2019) — "IntelliMerge: A Refactoring-Aware Software Merging Technique."** *PACMPL* 3(OOPSLA). DOI [10.1145/3360596](https://doi.org/10.1145/3360596). `[verified]` 58% fewer conflicts over 1,070 merge scenarios. "Stable node identity in a program graph, then merge" is structurally arbor's move.

## Structured diff and merge

- **Jean-Rémy Falleri, Floréal Morandat, Xavier Blanc, Matias Martinez, Martin Monperrus (ASE 2014) — "Fine-Grained and Accurate Source Code Differencing"** (GumTree). [HAL hal-01054552](https://hal.science/hal-01054552/document). `[verified — ACM DOI unconfirmed]` Edit scripts at AST granularity *including move actions*, versus text-granularity add-line/delete-line. Follow-on: **Falleri & Monperrus (ICSE 2024) — "Fine-grained, accurate and scalable source differencing,"** art. 231, DOI [10.1145/3597503.3639148](https://doi.org/10.1145/3597503.3639148) `[verified]`.
- **Sven Apel, Jörg Liebig, Benjamin Brandl, Christian Lengauer, Christian Kästner (ESEC/FSE 2011) — "Semistructured Merge: Rethinking Merge in Revision Control Systems."** pp. 190–200. DOI [10.1145/2025113.2025141](https://doi.org/10.1145/2025113.2025141). `[verified]` Directly relevant to p16/p17: semistructured merge *approximates* order-insensitivity where the language permits it; arbor's sorted-label-hash `Record` and `Sig` achieve it by construction.
- **Sven Apel, Olaf Leßenich, Christian Lengauer (ASE 2012) — "Structured Merge with Auto-Tuning: Balancing Precision and Performance"** (JDime). pp. 120–129. DOI [10.1145/2351676.2351694](https://doi.org/10.1145/2351676.2351694). `[verified]` **ASE 2012, not 2011**, and a different author list from the FSE 2011 paper above. Journal extension: *Automated Software Engineering*, DOI [10.1007/s10515-014-0151-5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10515-014-0151-5).
- **Guilherme Cavalcanti, Paulo Borba, Paola R. G. Accioly (OOPSLA 2017) — "Evaluating and Improving Semistructured Merge."** *PACMPL* 1(OOPSLA):59:1–59:27. DOI [10.1145/3133883](https://doi.org/10.1145/3133883). `[verified]` The methodology for measuring false-positive and false-negative conflicts — adopt this if arbor ever claims fewer conflicts.
- **Simon Larsén, Jean-Rémy Falleri, Benoit Baudry, Martin Monperrus (IEEE TSE 2022) — "Spork: Structured Merge for Java with Formatting Preservation."** DOI [10.1109/TSE.2022.3143766](https://doi.org/10.1109/TSE.2022.3143766); arXiv:2202.05329. `[verified]` arbor sidesteps the formatting problem entirely — layout is not in the hashed bytes. Worth naming as a design dividend rather than an accident.
- **Mergiraf** — tree-sitter-based syntax-aware Git merge driver, 33 languages. [mergiraf.org](https://mergiraf.org/), [related work](https://mergiraf.org/related-work.html), [LWN 2025](https://lwn.net/Articles/1042355/). `[not peer-reviewed — artifact; authorship UNVERIFIED]` The practical state of the art, shipping without a publication.
- **Benedikt Schesch, Ryan Featherman, Kenneth J. Yang, Ben R. Roberts, Michael D. Ernst (ASE 2024) — "Evaluation of Version Control Merge Tools."** pp. 831–843. DOI [10.1145/3691620.3695075](https://doi.org/10.1145/3691620.3695075); arXiv:2410.09934. `[verified]` Distinguishes *correct* from *incorrect* clean merges and prices the latter. **Read before any comparative merge claim.**

## CRDTs and replicated trees

- **Marc Shapiro, Nuno Preguiça, Carlos Baquero, Marek Zawirski (SSS 2011) — "Conflict-Free Replicated Data Types."** pp. 386–400. DOI [10.1007/978-3-642-24550-3_29](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-24550-3_29). `[verified]` Strong Eventual Consistency and the sufficient conditions for coordination-free convergence. Careful: arbor's *mutable namespace* is not automatically a CRDT.
- **Martin Kleppmann & Alastair R. Beresford (IEEE TPDS 2017) — "A Conflict-Free Replicated JSON Datatype."** 28(10):2733–2746. DOI [10.1109/TPDS.2017.2697382](https://doi.org/10.1109/TPDS.2017.2697382); arXiv:1608.03960. `[verified]` The algorithmic basis of **Automerge** (which has no separate canonical paper — cite this plus [automerge.org](https://automerge.org/)); the closest off-the-shelf datatype for arbor's namespace-as-nested-map.
- **Martin Kleppmann, Dominic P. Mulligan, Victor B. F. Gomes, Alastair R. Beresford (IEEE TPDS 2022) — "A Highly-Available Move Operation for Replicated Trees."** 33(7):1711–1724 (online 2021). DOI [10.1109/TPDS.2021.3118603](https://doi.org/10.1109/TPDS.2021.3118603); [PDF](https://martin.kleppmann.com/papers/move-op.pdf). `[verified]` **[Priority read.]** arbor's hierarchical dotted namespace under concurrent agentic writers *is* a replicated tree with moves and renames. This paper demonstrates actual bugs in Google Drive and Dropbox under concurrent moves and gives a correct algorithm. Filed in `../design/open-questions.md`.
- **Kleppmann, Gomes, Mulligan, Beresford (PaPoC 2019) — "Interleaving Anomalies in Collaborative Text Editors."** DOI [10.1145/3301419.3323972](https://doi.org/10.1145/3301419.3323972). `[verified]` The canonical "convergence is not correctness" citation — a distinction arbor's theorems must make explicitly.
- **Geoffrey Litt, Sarah Lim, Martin Kleppmann, Peter van Hardenberg (PACM HCI 2022) — "Peritext: A CRDT for Collaborative Rich Text Editing."** 6(CSCW2), art. 531. DOI [10.1145/3555644](https://doi.org/10.1145/3555644). `[verified]` **Second author is Sarah Lim (not "Lord"), and the venue is PACM HCI / CSCW — not OOPSLA or TOCHI.** Formatting annotations stored *outside* the text, attached to characters by unique ID — structurally identical to arbor's aspects-keyed-by-hash.
- **Kleppmann, Adam Wiggins, Peter van Hardenberg, Mark McGranaghan (Onward! 2019) — "Local-First Software: You Own Your Data, in Spite of the Cloud."** DOI [10.1145/3359591.3359737](https://doi.org/10.1145/3359591.3359737). `[verified]` The seven ideals; established vocabulary for "forking = cheap copied views" and the offline-agent story.

## Names, binding, and identity

- **N. G. de Bruijn (1972) — "Lambda calculus notation with nameless dummies, a tool for automatic formula manipulation, with application to the Church-Rosser theorem."** *Indagationes Mathematicae* 75(5):381–392 (also KNAW Proc. ser. A vol. 75). [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/1385725872900340). `[verified]` **Volume discrepancy:** both "vol. 34" and "vol. 75" citations circulate; the JSL review lists both. `formalism/paper/references.bib` currently says 34. The primary citation for the *representation*; for the hashing result, cite Maziarz et al. in `01-content-addressing.md`.
- **Murdoch J. Gabbay & Andrew M. Pitts (2002) — "A New Approach to Abstract Syntax with Variable Binding."** *Formal Aspects of Computing* 13(3–5):341–363. DOI [10.1007/s001650200016](https://doi.org/10.1007/s001650200016). `[verified]` The alternative to de Bruijn for `formalism/agda/` — names kept but quotiented. Worth an explicit decision entry given the mechanization is not yet written.
- **Venera Arnaoudova, Laleh Mousavi Eshkevari, Massimiliano Di Penta, Rocco Oliveto, Giuliano Antoniol, Yann-Gaël Guéhéneuc (IEEE TSE 2014) — "REPENT: Analyzing the Nature of Identifier Renamings."** 40(5):502–532. [PDF](https://mdipenta.github.io/files/TSE2312942.pdf). `[verified]` The best citable evidence for the "brittle names" premise: renaming is frequent, semantically varied, and currently only recoverable by inference.
- **Xiaoxia Ren, Fenil Shah, Frank Tip, Barbara G. Ryder, Ophelia Chesley (OOPSLA 2004) — "Chianti: A Tool for Change Impact Analysis of Java Programs."** pp. 432–448. [PDF](https://prolangs.cs.vt.edu/refs/docs/oopsla04.pdf). `[verified — DOI unconfirmed]` Decomposes a version difference into atomic changes and reports impact as affected tests — the analysis-derived version of what `Store.callers_of` answers exactly.
- **Yi Li, Julia Rubin, Marsha Chechik (ASE 2015) — "Semantic Slicing of Software Version Histories."** DOI [10.1109/ASE.2015.47](https://doi.org/10.1109/ASE.2015.47). `[verified]` **This is Li/Rubin/Chechik, not "Li, Chandra, Rountev," and there is no ICSE 2017 paper titled "Automated Semantic History Slicing."** Follow-ons: "Precise semantic history slicing through dynamic delta refinement," ASE 2016, DOI [10.1145/2970276.2970336](https://doi.org/10.1145/2970276.2970336) (extended in *AUSE*, DOI [10.1007/s10515-019-00260-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10515-019-00260-8)); CSLICERCLOUD, ICSE 2018 demo.

## Gaps and negative findings

- **Namespace-as-replicated-tree is unaddressed.** Filed in `../design/open-questions.md`. Branching is a stated non-goal in `00-overview.md`, but agentic concurrency arrives before branching does.
- **Behavioural breaking changes remain the unsolved class** in every empirical study. That is exactly where eval-stability is novel and also hardest to state.
- **The motivation must not rest on breakage frequency.** Ochoa et al. show most breaking changes never reach clients. Certainty and verification cost are the defensible ground.
- **Mergiraf**, the practical state of the art in structured merge, has no paper and unverified authorship.
- **Convergence is not correctness** (Kleppmann et al., PaPoC 2019). Any future claim that arbor "cannot conflict" must say which of the two it means.
