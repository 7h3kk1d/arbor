# Caching and Incremental Computation Keyed on Content

**Status:** Index. Sourced 2026-08-03. Annotations are notes and observations, not substrate policy — see `00-index.md` §"How to read this".

## Why this theme

arbor's aspect store is a cache keyed on the transitive content hash of its inputs, and `formalism/paper/arbor-core.tex`'s eval-stability theorem is a statement about that cache. This literature already has vocabulary, taxonomies, and proof shapes for exactly that object — using them is cheaper and more convincing than inventing terms.

Four things here change what arbor should write down:

1. **Build Systems à la Carte locates arbor precisely.** In its scheduler × rebuilder grid, arbor is the *deep constructive trace / cloud build* corner. That is a one-sentence positioning arbor currently lacks.
2. **Nominal Adapton is arbor's identity design, from the other side.** Structural memoization alone is insufficient; you need first-class *names* to say which cache location a computation reuses. arbor's hashes are precise names by construction — and **p10/p11's mint marks are the nominal-name channel that hashes cannot provide.** Minted mark ≈ nominal name, content hash ≈ structural key. That is the sharpest available statement of arbor's identity design.
3. **Co-contextual type rules would extend caching below the definition root.** Right now the type-check aspect must be keyed on a *definition root's* hash, because a de Bruijn subterm's type depends on its enclosing binders and its hash alone does not determine it. Deriving `e ⟹ (T, context requirements)` instead of `Γ ⊢ e : T` makes the hash sufficient. This is the one item in the survey suggesting a concrete change.
4. **Porter et al. (OOPSLA 2025) owns the granularity gap arbor cannot reach.** Content hashing gives cross-session, cross-machine, forever-valid reuse at *definition* granularity — and nothing at all keystroke-to-keystroke within one definition, because every keystroke is a fresh hash. p9's "ingest synchronously on every keystroke" is where arbor pays that price.

## Build systems

- **Andrey Mokhov, Neil Mitchell, Simon Peyton Jones — "Build Systems à la Carte."** *PACMPL* 2(ICFP), art. 79, 2018, DOI [10.1145/3236774](https://doi.org/10.1145/3236774); extended as "Build systems à la carte: Theory and practice," *JFP* 30, art. e11, 2020, DOI [10.1017/S0956796820000088](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0956796820000088). `[verified]` **[Priority read — the JFP version.]** Decomposes any build system into scheduler × rebuilder over a small grid: dirty bit, verifying traces, constructive traces, deep constructive traces. arbor is the deep-constructive-trace corner, hence shareable. Supplies minimality and correctness definitions to state eval-stability *against* rather than inventing terms.
- **Sebastian Erdweg, Moritz Lichter, Manuel Weiel (OOPSLA 2015) — "A Sound and Optimal Incremental Build System with Dynamic Dependencies"** (pluto). pp. 89–106. DOI [10.1145/2814270.2814316](https://doi.org/10.1145/2814270.2814316). `[verified]` **[Priority read — for proof structure.]** Sound (result equals from-scratch) and optimal (no redundant work), with builders discovering dependencies at run time and declaring *content-level* requirements. **The nearest existing proof of the theorem shape arbor wants.**
- **Gabriël Konat, Michael Steindorfer, Sebastian Erdweg, Eelco Visser (2018) — "PIE: A Domain-Specific Language for Interactive Software Development Pipelines."** *The Art, Science, and Engineering of Programming* 2(3), art. 9. DOI [10.22152/programming-journal.org/2018/2/9](https://doi.org/10.22152/programming-journal.org/2018/2/9). `[verified]` **A journal article, not a conference paper** — a common miscitation. Companion: **Konat, Erdweg, Visser (ASE 2018) — "Scalable Incremental Building with Dynamic Task Dependencies,"** DOI [10.1145/3238147.3238196](https://doi.org/10.1145/3238147.3238196) `[verified]`. Persistence-across-restarts is engineered here; arbor gets it free from immutability.
- **Neil Mitchell (ICFP 2012) — "Shake Before Building: Replacing Make with Haskell."** pp. 55–66. DOI [10.1145/2364527.2364538](https://doi.org/10.1145/2364527.2364538). `[verified]`
- **Mitchell, Kiefer, Iborra, Lau, Duggal, Siebenhandl, Pickering, Zimmerman (IFL 2020) — "Building an Integrated Development Environment (IDE) on top of a Build System."** DOI [10.1145/3462172.3462180](https://doi.org/10.1145/3462172.3462180). `[verified]` ghcide/HLS: **an IDE *is* a build system.** The direct precedent for arbor's framing of type-check, evaluation, and tests as *aspects* rather than compiler phases — and honest about where the abstraction leaks: diagnostics, cancellation, eviction.

## Production caches — and the unsoundness to avoid

- **Bazel remote caching** — action cache plus content-addressable store, both SHA-256-keyed, shared across a team. [bazel.build/remote/caching](https://bazel.build/remote/caching). `[not peer-reviewed — artifact]` **Directly actionable:** Bazel does not hash tools outside the workspace, so two machines with different compilers collide on one action key. arbor's aspect keys must include the evaluator/checker version — p6's `stlc:type-check:v1` and `arith-to-lc-church:translate:v1` procedure-ids suggest `../design/02-definitions-and-derived-data.md` already does this; **this is the citation for why it matters.**
- **NixOS RFC 062, "Content-addressed paths"** plus the Tweag implementation series. [RFC](https://github.com/NixOS/rfcs/blob/master/rfcs/0062-content-addressed-paths.md), [Tweag](https://www.tweag.io/blog/2021-12-02-nix-cas-4/). `[not peer-reviewed — artifact]` The **early-cutoff** mechanism: rebuild is skipped when a *resolved* derivation matches something already known. Early cutoff is what α-canonicalization buys arbor for free; Nix had to bolt it on.
- **ccache** ([ccache.dev](https://ccache.dev/)) and **sccache** ([github.com/mozilla/sccache](https://github.com/mozilla/sccache)). `[not peer-reviewed — artifact]` ccache's *direct mode* is a manifest-of-dependencies design; arbor's DAG *is* the manifest, so the split collapses. sccache is the shared/cloud half of arbor's claim — with the caveat that absolute paths must match by default, i.e. real deployments leak non-content into the key.
- **Unison's test and type caching.** [The big idea](https://www.unison-lang.org/docs/the-big-idea/), [Testing](https://www.unison-lang.org/docs/usage-topics/testing/). `[no publication exists]` Deterministic test results cached and re-run only when a hash in the dependency graph changes. **p17's `test` aspect is this feature.**
- **Google, "Build in the Cloud"** (4-part series, 2011). `[UNVERIFIED — URLs exist but article bodies were not read]` Historical origin only; cite Bazel's docs for mechanism.

## Self-adjusting and demand-driven computation

- **Umut A. Acar, Guy E. Blelloch, Robert Harper (POPL 2002 / TOPLAS 2006) — "Adaptive Functional Programming."** pp. 247–259; *TOPLAS* 28(6):990–1034. DOI [10.1145/1186632.1186634](https://doi.org/10.1145/1186632.1186634). `[verified]` And **Acar (2005) — "Self-Adjusting Computation,"** PhD thesis, CMU-CS-05-129 `[verified]`. **The reference point for what arbor deliberately does *not* do:** there is no change propagation because there is no mutation — an edit is a new hash. State this explicitly; reviewers will ask.
- **Matthew A. Hammer, Khoo Yit Phang, Michael Hicks, Jeffrey S. Foster (PLDI 2014) — "Adapton: Composable, Demand-Driven Incremental Computation."** pp. 156–166. DOI [10.1145/2594291.2594324](https://doi.org/10.1145/2594291.2594324). `[verified]` **Authors are Hammer/Khoo/Hicks/Foster — not Acar or Van Horn.** Demand-driven is arbor's model: an aspect is computed when queried, cache consulted first.
- **Hammer, Dunfield, Headley, Labich, Foster, Hicks, Van Horn (OOPSLA 2015) — "Incremental Computation with Names"** (Nominal Adapton). pp. 748–766. DOI [10.1145/2814270.2814305](https://doi.org/10.1145/2814270.2814305); arXiv:1503.07792. `[verified]` **[Priority read — the most important item in this theme.]** Structural memoization alone is insufficient; you need first-class *names* to identify which cache location a computation should reuse, with a proved from-scratch consistency result.

  **The careful comparison for arbor.** Nominal Adapton's names are programmer-chosen and must be allocated *freshly and uniquely at run time*, because two live entries under one name is unsound. arbor's names are canonical functions of content, so (a) they cannot collide unsoundly — equal name implies equal content implies equal result, which is why eval-stability is *provable* rather than engineered; and (b) they are globally meaningful, so the cache is shareable across machines, which no Adapton-family system claims. The cost is the mirror image: arbor cannot say "same cache location, different content" — which is exactly what p10/p11's **mint marks** reintroduce as a separate identity channel. **Minted mark ≈ nominal name; content hash ≈ structural key.**
- **Hammer, Dunfield, Economou, Narasimhamurthy (2016) — "Refinement Types for Precisely Named Cache Locations."** arXiv:1610.00097. `[verified as preprint; peer-reviewed venue UNVERIFIED]` (Headley is *not* an author here.) Defines the **precise name problem** — statically verifying names are used without aliasing or unintended reuse. The cleanest statement of an obligation arbor discharges by construction. Follow-on: **Fungi**, arXiv:1808.07826, 2018 `[verified as preprint; venue UNVERIFIED]`.
- **Matthew A. Hammer & Umut A. Acar (ISMM 2008) — "Memory Management for Self-Adjusting Computation."** pp. 51–60. DOI [10.1145/1375634.1375642](https://doi.org/10.1145/1375634.1375642). `[verified]` **Flagged as a live risk rather than a read.** "Valid forever" converts a correctness problem into an unbounded-growth problem, and arbor has no eviction story. Filed in `../design/open-questions.md`.
- **Yufei Cai, Paolo G. Giarrusso, Tillmann Rendel, Klaus Ostermann (PLDI 2014) — "A Theory of Changes for Higher-Order Languages: Incrementalizing λ-Calculi by Static Differentiation."** pp. 145–155. DOI [10.1145/2594291.2594304](https://doi.org/10.1145/2594291.2594304); arXiv:1312.0658. `[verified]` Mechanized in **Agda** for a family of simply-typed λ-calculi — the closest methodological template for `formalism/agda/`'s roadmap.

## Foundations of memoization

- **William Pugh & Tim Teitelbaum (POPL 1989) — "Incremental Computation via Function Caching."** pp. 315–328. DOI [10.1145/75277.75305](https://doi.org/10.1145/75277.75305). `[verified]` The origin of "make the program pure, then cache calls by argument value" — arbor's direct ancestor.
- **Donald Michie (1968) — "'Memo' Functions and Machine Learning."** *Nature* 218(5136):19–22. DOI [10.1038/218019a0](https://doi.org/10.1038/218019a0). `[verified]` **Michie alone, not Bellman.**
- **Martín Abadi, Butler Lampson, Jean-Jacques Lévy (ICFP 1996) — "Analysis and Caching of Dependencies."** pp. 83–91. DOI [10.1145/232627.232638](https://doi.org/10.1145/232627.232638). `[verified]` Caches on *which parts of a term contributed to the result* rather than the whole term — the theoretical basis for a cache key coarser than the full content hash.

## Incremental type checking

- **Sebastian Erdweg, Oliver Bračevac, Edlira Kuci, Matthias Krebs, Mira Mezini (OOPSLA 2015) — "A Co-Contextual Formulation of Type Rules and Its Application to Incremental Type Checking."** pp. 880–897. DOI [10.1145/2814270.2814277](https://doi.org/10.1145/2814270.2814277). `[verified]` **[Priority read — the most actionable item in the survey.]** Instead of `Γ ⊢ e : T`, derive `e ⟹ (T, context requirements)`. Removing the input context makes subterm checking independent and therefore cacheable. **Right now arbor's type-check aspect is keyed on a definition root's hash**, because a de Bruijn subterm's meaning depends on its enclosing binders and its hash alone does not determine its type. Co-contextual rules are the principled fix: the cached aspect becomes `(type, requirements)`, which *is* determined by the hash. Companion: **Kuci, Erdweg, Bračevac, Bejleri, Mezini (ECOOP 2017) — "A Co-contextual Type Checker for Featherweight Java,"** LIPIcs 74, pp. 18:1–18:26, DOI [10.4230/LIPIcs.ECOOP.2017.18](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ECOOP.2017.18) `[verified]` — handles the class table, which is exactly a global name→definition map, i.e. arbor's namespace.
- **Aron Zwaan, Hendrik van Antwerpen, Eelco Visser (OOPSLA 2022) — "Incremental Type-Checking for Free: Using Scope Graphs to Derive Incremental Type-Checkers."** *PACMPL* 6(OOPSLA2):424–448. DOI [10.1145/3563303](https://doi.org/10.1145/3563303). `[verified]` **This is Zwaan/van Antwerpen/Visser (Statix, TU Delft) — not Pacak/Erdweg.** Splits checking into context-free and context-sensitive halves and uses *query confirmation* to decide which name-resolution answers changed. **That split maps directly onto arbor's structural hash (context-free, cacheable forever) versus namespace resolution (context-sensitive, must re-run)** — and scope graphs are the direct competitor model to `../design/04-naming-layer.md`.
- **André Pacak, Sebastian Erdweg, Tamás Szabó (OOPSLA 2020) — "A Systematic Approach to Deriving Incremental Type Checkers."** *PACMPL* 4(OOPSLA), art. 127. DOI [10.1145/3428195](https://doi.org/10.1145/3428195). `[verified]` **Not titled "Incremental type-checking for free" — that is Zwaan et al. above.** Compiles inference rules to Datalog and gets incrementality from an incremental solver. Related: **Pacak & Erdweg (ECOOP 2022) — "Functional Programming with Datalog,"** DOI [10.4230/LIPIcs.ECOOP.2022.7](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ECOOP.2022.7); **Pacak, Szabó, Erdweg (GPCE 2022) — "Incremental Processing of Structured Data in Datalog,"** DOI [10.1145/3564719.3568686](https://doi.org/10.1145/3564719.3568686). `[verified]`
- **Porter, Kirisame, Wei, Panchekha, Omar (OOPSLA 2025) — "Incremental Bidirectional Typing via Order Maintenance."** *PACMPL* 9(OOPSLA2). Distinguished Paper. DOI [10.1145/3763117](https://doi.org/10.1145/3763117); arXiv:[2504.08946](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.08946). `[verified — author list and ordering confirmed against Crossref 2026-08-03]` **[Priority read — the paper arbor must be positioned against, not merely alongside.]** Propagates type updates through a marked program in response to fine-grained edits, using order-maintenance structures for binder→use pointers. **It solves precisely the inner loop content hashing cannot reach.**
- **Kirisame, Porter, Yang, Zhao, Wu, Wei, Omar, Panchekha (2026) — "Incremental Live Programming via Shortcut Memoization"** (Chordata). arXiv:[2603.19560](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19560). `[verified as preprint; venue not yet established]` An incremental interpreter that learns shortcuts from executions of *previous versions* and replays them on structurally similar fragments (13.03× speedup, 19.97× memory). **The most direct challenge to arbor's eval-cache design:** arbor hits only on exact hash equality; Chordata hits on structural similarity *across versions*. Since p11 already has mint threads linking versions of one definition, this is the paper that says what that link could do at evaluation time.
- **Niko Matsakis (PLISS 2019) — "Responsive Compilers."** [Slides](https://nikomatsakis.github.io/pliss-2019/responsive-compilers.html). `[not peer-reviewed — talk]` The canonical statement of "compilation is a demand-driven query graph, not a batch pipeline." Supporting engineering docs: [rustc incremental compilation in detail](https://rustc-dev-guide.rust-lang.org/queries/incremental-compilation-in-detail.html) — rustc keys on *stable hashes* deliberately so spans and ordering do not invalidate, a hand-built approximation of arbor's canonicalization; [Salsa](https://github.com/salsa-rs/salsa) and rust-analyzer's ["Durable Incrementality"](https://rust-analyzer.github.io/blog/2023/07/24/durable-incrementality.html), self-described as Adapton-inspired. `[not peer-reviewed — software]`

## Adjacent

## Gaps and negative findings

- **No aspect eviction story.** "Valid forever" is an unbounded-growth problem (Hammer & Acar, ISMM 2008). Filed in `../design/open-questions.md`.
- **arbor's caching stops at the definition root.** Co-contextual rules are the principled route below it; nothing in arbor's docs currently acknowledges the limit.
- **arbor has nothing for the keystroke-to-keystroke inner loop**, which Porter et al. solve directly. p9's synchronous-ingest-per-keystroke is where that cost lands.
- **No citable Roslyn or Hejlsberg incremental-compilation design document exists.** Searches turned up only a ResearchGate preprint with inconsistent attribution and an IJSRA article — neither citable. "Compiler as a service" is Microsoft product terminology. Drop the lead, or cite the [TypeScript `--incremental` docs](https://www.typescriptlang.org/tsconfig/incremental.html) for the persisted-program-graph pattern only.
- Four items are `[UNVERIFIED]` or venue-unconfirmed: Google's "Build in the Cloud" bodies, the Refinement Types and Fungi venues, and Porter et al.'s author ordering.
