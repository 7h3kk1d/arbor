# Benchmarks, Challenges, and Evaluation Frameworks

**Status:** Index. Sourced 2026-08-06. Annotations are notes and observations, not substrate policy — see `00-index.md` §"How to read this".

## Why this theme

Two questions this file serves. First: if a consolidated arbor artifact is ever framed as a *toolkit or benchmark for working with program terms*, what is the prior art for how the PL community builds shared benchmarks, and which genre should it imitate? Second: by what existing instruments would arbor itself be evaluated?

The prior-art space splits cleanly into four genres, and the headline finding is that **none of them contains a benchmark for program stores or term-manipulation toolkits** — the cell arbor would occupy is empty (see §"Gaps and negative findings").

A genre lesson worth stating up front: **challenge problems outlive task-suite infrastructure.** POPLmark is still being cited and re-run twenty years on; the Language Workbench Challenge's website is dead and its post-2013 editions left no archival record. If arbor publishes in this genre, the durable form is a paper stating problems, not a website hosting tasks.

## Genre 1 — Task assignments: the Language Workbench Challenge

- **Erdweg, van der Storm, Völter, Tratt, Bosman, Cook, Gerritsen, Hulshout, Kelly, Loh, Konat, Molina, Palatnik, Pohjonen, E. Schindler, K. Schindler, Solmi, Vergu, Visser, van der Vlist, Wachsmuth, van der Woning (COMLAN 2015) — "Evaluating and Comparing Language Workbenches: Existing Results and Benchmarks for the Future."** *Computer Languages, Systems & Structures* 44:24–47. DOI [10.1016/j.cl.2015.08.007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cl.2015.08.007). `[verified]` **[Priority read if the benchmark framing proceeds.]** Exactly **22 authors**. Extends the SLE 2013 feature comparison (already in `02-structure-editors.md`) by documenting the LWC'11/'12/'13 task assignments as reusable benchmarks and proposing future benchmark dimensions. **This paper is the archival home of the challenge** — the assignments live here, not on the challenge site.
- **The Language Workbench Challenge series itself.** `[no publication exists — for the series]` No archival proceedings; the peer-reviewed record is SLE 2013 + COMLAN 2015 only. Later editions ran as non-archival co-located workshops (e.g., LWC@SLE at SPLASH 2016 — talks and demos, no indexed proceedings). The site `languageworkbenches.net` **no longer resolves** (DNS dead as of 2026-08); if it must be cited, cite an archive.org capture as a defunct artifact.

## Genre 2 — Challenge problems: mechanized metatheory

The genre closest to what arbor could publish: a small set of precisely-stated problems that expose where infrastructures differ, with the community invited to respond.

- **Aydemir, Bohannon, Fairbairn, Foster, Pierce, Sewell, Vytiniotis, Washburn, Weirich, Zdancewic (TPHOLs 2005) — "Mechanized Metatheory for the Masses: The PoplMark Challenge."** LNCS 3603, pp. 50–65. DOI [10.1007/11541868_4](https://doi.org/10.1007/11541868_4). `[verified]` Benchmark problems over System F<: metatheory — binding, variable-arity syntax, induction — designed to measure progress in mechanization. The genre-defining paper, still generating responses two decades on.
- **Abel, Allais, Hameer, Pientka, Momigliano, Schäfer, Stark (JFP 2019) — "POPLMark Reloaded: Mechanizing Proofs by Logical Relations."** *Journal of Functional Programming* 29:e19. DOI [10.1017/S0956796819000170](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0956796819000170). `[verified]` (Spellings that circulate wrong: **Guillaume Allais**, **Schäfer**.) The successor challenge — strong normalization of STLC via logical relations — explicitly comparing *binding infrastructures* across proof assistants. Directly relevant to `formalism/`: if arbor-core is ever mechanized, this is the current test of the binding machinery it would choose, and the representation-independence proof sketched for the abstraction line is exactly a logical-relations proof of the kind this challenge exercises. Companion artifact: [poplmark-reloaded.github.io](https://poplmark-reloaded.github.io/) `[not peer-reviewed — artifact]`.
- **Felty, Momigliano, Pientka (JAR 2015) — "The Next 700 Challenge Problems for Reasoning with Higher-Order Abstract Syntax Representations: Part 2—A Survey."** *Journal of Automated Reasoning* 55(4):307–372. DOI [10.1007/s10817-015-9327-3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10817-015-9327-3). `[verified]` The ORBI benchmark suite run across Twelf, Beluga, Abella, Hybrid. **Part 1 — the ORBI infrastructure spec — was never published**: arXiv:[1503.06095](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.06095) only `[not peer-reviewed — preprint]`. Companion: **"Benchmarks for Reasoning with Syntax Trees Containing Binders and Contexts of Assumptions,"** *MSCS* 28(9):1507–1540, 2018, DOI [10.1017/S0960129517000093](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0960129517000093) `[verified]`. Together with POPLmark these establish that *name binding* is the community's canonical benchmark axis — the axis arbor's canonicalized-de-Bruijn hashing (`01-content-addressing.md`, Maziarz et al.) sits on.
- **Klein, Clements, Dimoulas, Eastlund, Felleisen, Flatt, McCarthy, Rafkind, Tobin-Hochstadt, Findler (POPL 2012) — "Run Your Research: On the Effectiveness of Lightweight Mechanization."** pp. 285–296. DOI [10.1145/2103656.2103691](https://doi.org/10.1145/2103656.2103691). `[verified]` Re-mechanized nine published POPL/ICFP semantics in Redex and found errors in **all nine**. The methodological argument for executable semantics — and the citable support for arbor's prototypes-as-evidence posture: a claim exercised by a running prototype is worth more than one stated on paper.

## Genre 3 — Performance suites

- **Marr, Daloze, Mössenböck (DLS 2016) — "Cross-Language Compiler Benchmarking: Are We Fast Yet?"** pp. 120–131. DOI [10.1145/2989225.2989232](https://doi.org/10.1145/2989225.2989232). `[verified]` Fourteen benchmarks over a fixed *core language* of common abstractions implemented identically across six languages — the methodological answer to CLBG's "same algorithm" problem, and the relevant precedent for any cross-language performance claim in a multi-language store (`06-multi-language.md`). Suite: [github.com/smarr/are-we-fast-yet](https://github.com/smarr/are-we-fast-yet) `[not peer-reviewed — software]`.
- **The Computer Language Benchmarks Game.** [benchmarksgame-team.pages.debian.net/benchmarksgame](https://benchmarksgame-team.pages.debian.net/benchmarksgame/) (maintainer Isaac Gouy). `[not peer-reviewed — software/artifact]` ~13 toy programs × ~30 languages; the site itself concedes the programs are unrealistic microbenchmarks. **No dedicated peer-reviewed critique exists** — the citable critique is embedded in Marr et al. above. Peer-reviewed *use* as a corpus: **Pereira, Couto, Ribeiro, Rua, Cunha, Fernandes, Saraiva (SLE 2017) — "Energy Efficiency Across Programming Languages,"** pp. 256–267, DOI [10.1145/3136014.3136031](https://doi.org/10.1145/3136014.3136031) `[verified]`.
- **Rosetta Code.** [rosettacode.org](https://rosettacode.org/). `[not peer-reviewed — artifact]` Community wiki of tasks solved in many languages. Verified academic use: **Nanz & Furia (ICSE 2015) — "A Comparative Study of Programming Languages in Rosetta Code,"** pp. 778–788, DOI [10.1109/ICSE.2015.90](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICSE.2015.90); arXiv:[1409.0252](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0252) `[verified]`. Relevant less as a benchmark than as prior art for a *multi-language corpus of small aligned definitions* — which is what a seeded multi-language arbor store would be.
- **Stephanie Weirich — lambda-n-ways.** [github.com/sweirich/lambda-n-ways](https://github.com/sweirich/lambda-n-ways). `[not peer-reviewed — software/artifact]` Benchmarks capture-avoiding substitution and normalization across dozens of Haskell binding representations (named, de Bruijn, locally nameless, NbE, …). **The closest existing thing to a term-manipulation-library benchmark**, and directly relevant to arbor's implementation choices: canonicalization-then-hash is a binding representation with costs this suite's axes would measure. Unpublished — which is itself part of the empty-cell finding below.

## Genre 4 — Environment evaluation: frameworks, not benchmarks

What exists for comparing programming *environments* is qualitative vocabulary and process methodology; no shared task suite exists.

- **Green & Petre (JVLC 1996) — "Usability Analysis of Visual Programming Environments: A 'Cognitive Dimensions' Framework."** 7(2):131–174. DOI [10.1006/jvlc.1996.0009](https://doi.org/10.1006/jvlc.1996.0009). `[verified]` The classic qualitative vocabulary (viscosity, premature commitment, hidden dependencies, …). Pairs with Jakubovic–Edwards–Petricek's Technical Dimensions (`09-scid.md`) as the two off-the-shelf instruments for positioning arbor against Unison, Hazel, MPS, and Smalltalk — one cognitive, one architectural. "Hidden dependencies" is precisely what `Store.callers_of` and the no-silent-breakage posture attack.
- **Coblenz, Kambhatla, Koronkevich, Wise, Barnaby, Sunshine, Aldrich, Myers (TOCHI 2021) — "PLIERS: A Process that Integrates User-Centered Methods into Programming Language Design."** 28(4), art. 28. DOI [10.1145/3452379](https://doi.org/10.1145/3452379). `[verified]` A design *process*, not a benchmark — the current statement of how to evaluate language designs with users, relevant if an interface prototype ever runs a study.
- **Berger, Völter, Jensen, Dangprasert, Siegmund (FSE 2016) — "Efficiency of Projectional Editing: A Controlled Experiment."** pp. 763–774. DOI [10.1145/2950290.2950315](https://doi.org/10.1145/2950290.2950315). `[verified]` MPS vs. Eclipse CDT, 19 participants. A one-off experiment design, not a reusable suite — and the nearest thing that exists to editor-tooling evaluation at all. Belongs equally to `02-structure-editors.md`.
- **Berger, Blackburn, Hauswirth, Hicks (ACM SIGPLAN EC, 2018) — "Empirical Evaluation Guidelines."** [sigplan.org/Resources/EmpiricalEvaluation](https://www.sigplan.org/Resources/EmpiricalEvaluation/) + one-page checklist PDF (v. 2018-10-26). `[not peer-reviewed — checklist/web resource; no archival version exists]` The community's stated bar for any empirical claim a paper makes — the checklist to run any future sharing/cache-hit measurements against.

## Where it plugs in

- **The toolkit/benchmark framing question** (raised in dissertation planning, not yet in any design doc): the four genres are the option space, and the genre lesson — challenges outlive task suites — is the design input. Candidate challenge-problem material arbor already owns: rename-at-scale with no stored-name invariant, edit-propagation with visible breakage, cross-language migration of a definition DAG, concurrent-edit convergence, cache survival under edit scripts. Stated as problems, these are publishable independent of arbor's own implementation.
- `formalism/paper/arbor-core.tex` — POPLmark Reloaded and ORBI are the mechanization context (binding infrastructure choice); Run Your Research is the methodological citation for executable-semantics-as-evidence.
- `01-content-addressing.md` (Maziarz et al.) and the de Bruijn conventions — lambda-n-ways is the performance lens on the same design axis.
- `06-multi-language.md` — Are We Fast Yet's core-language methodology is the precedent for any claim comparing arbor's languages; Rosetta Code is prior art for an aligned multi-language corpus.
- `02-structure-editors.md` — Berger et al. (FSE 2016) and the no-editing-benchmark finding below; the SLE 2013 workbench survey already lives there.
- `09-scid.md` — Cognitive Dimensions pairs with Technical Dimensions as the environment-comparison vocabulary.

## Gaps and negative findings

- **No benchmark or challenge suite exists for program stores or term-manipulation toolkits.** The space splits into workbench task assignments (LWC), metatheory proof challenges (POPLmark/ORBI — proof-assistant-level, not library-level), performance suites (CLBG, Are We Fast Yet), and one unpublished library-level binding microbenchmark (lambda-n-ways). A benchmark for *stores of program terms* — ingest, canonicalization, rename, edit propagation, cache behavior — sits in an empty cell. Budget-limited search: a failure to find, not a proof of absence.
- **No benchmark exists for structure editors or program-editing tooling.** Only one-off controlled experiments (Berger et al. FSE 2016). Recent "code-editing benchmark" search hits are all LLM instruction-following datasets — a different field.
- **No standard task suite for comparing programming environments** — only qualitative frameworks (Cognitive Dimensions, Technical Dimensions) and process methodology (PLIERS).
- **No archival record of the LWC series** beyond SLE 2013 + COMLAN 2015; `languageworkbenches.net` is DNS-dead as of 2026-08.
- **No dedicated peer-reviewed critique of the Computer Language Benchmarks Game**; critiques live inside other papers (Marr et al.) and blogs (Marceau 2009 — `[not peer-reviewed — blog]`).
- **The ORBI spec (Part 1) is arXiv-only**; the peer-reviewed anchors are the JAR 2015 survey and the MSCS 2018 paper.
- **The SIGPLAN Empirical Evaluation Guidelines have no archival publication** — cite as a web resource with the author list and 2018 date.
