\title{\textbf{arbor-core}\\[2pt]
\large A formalism of the untyped $\lambda$-fragment of the arbor substrate:\\
content-addressed store, naming layer, evaluation cache, and an edit calculus}
\author{The arbor project}
\date{Draft --- \today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We formalize the smallest self-contained slice of the arbor substrate: the untyped
$\lambda$-calculus stored in a content-addressed term store, a namespace that maps names to
hashes, a derived evaluation cache, and an \emph{edit calculus} of binding operations and
explicit migrations. The development is faithful to two arbor prototypes --- \texttt{p4}
(untyped $\lambda$) and \texttt{p11} (migration) --- and to the design documents
\texttt{03-content-addressing} and \texttt{04-naming-layer}. Its purpose is to state precisely,
and in a form ready for mechanization, \emph{what the substrate guarantees and why
content-addressing earns its complexity}. Everything reduces to one structural fact:
evaluation is a pure function of the term store and a hash, and the store is immutable and
monotone. From it we derive two dual metatheorems --- \emph{no silent breakage} (name edits
never change the meaning of stored code) and \emph{evaluation stability} (a computed result is
valid forever, so migration re-evaluates only genuinely new definitions).
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and posture}\label{sec:scope}

This is the first artifact in a formalism developed the way the arbor prototypes are:
\emph{start small, work up}. We take the untyped $\lambda$-calculus --- enough to exhibit
content-addressing's payoff, not so much that types obscure it --- and model four pieces of
substrate state:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item a content-addressed \textbf{term store} $\Store$ (\cref{sec:store});
  \item a \textbf{namespace} $\Names$ mapping names to hashes (\cref{sec:naming});
  \item a derived \textbf{evaluation cache} $\Cache$ (\cref{sec:cache});
  \item an \textbf{edit calculus} of transitions on $\Config{\Store}{\Names}{\Cache}{\Hist}$,
  where $\Hist$ is an append-only binding history (\cref{sec:edit}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Two theorems are the point.} The design's whole justification is packed into a dual
pair, proved in \cref{sec:meta}:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \emph{No silent breakage} (\cref{thm:nsb}): editing names can never change the meaning
  of a stored term.
  \item \emph{Evaluation stability / no re-evaluation} (\cref{thm:stability}): a once-computed
  evaluation result is valid in every future store; store growth and migration never invalidate
  it.
\end{itemize}
Both follow from one fact the formalism makes syntactic: \textbf{$\Downarrow$ is a function of
$(\Store, h)$ and $\Store$ only ever grows}. Names and the edit calculus live \emph{beside}
$\Store$, never inside it.

\paragraph{What is in, what is out.} Modeling rationale lives in
\texttt{formalism/decisions.md}; the paper states the choices and does not re-argue them.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Migration strategies are in}: pin / follow / explicit as propagation scopes
  over a whole-store rewrite, committed by an atomic multi-rebind (\cref{sec:edit}).
  \item \textbf{The evaluation cache is in}: a derived, hash-valued aspect; ``never
  re-evaluate'' is \cref{cor:cache}, not a slogan.
  \item \textbf{Mints (creation-identity) are out}: hence no definition sort --- naming a
  closed stored term is the only assertion of definition-hood, and migration propagates
  structurally rather than by unit (\cref{rem:nosort}).
  \item \textbf{Types are out}: the migration gate is consequently total here
  (\cref{rem:gate}); its interesting failure modes arrive with the typed successor.
  \item \textbf{$\nRef{h}$ is in}, though \texttt{p4} inlines closed subterms: without an
  explicit reference node there are no cross-definition edges and migration is vacuous.
\end{itemize}
Also deferred: the general aspect store, translation, holes, branching/merging, hierarchical
names, orphan GC --- tracked in \texttt{formalism/open-questions.md}.

\section{Part I --- The object language}\label{sec:lang}

Arbor keeps two syntaxes: a \emph{surface} syntax with names, produced by parsers and shown to
users, and an internal \emph{core} syntax that is name-free by construction. The split is what
makes ``names never appear in stored programs'' a statement about \emph{types}, not a runtime
check.

\subsection{Core terms (name-free, de Bruijn)}

\begin{definition}[Core terms]\label{def:core}
\src{ast.re, +\,ref} Core terms use de Bruijn indices for bound variables and an explicit
reference leaf for cross-definition uses:
\[
  t,u \;::=\; \Var{i} \;\mid\; \Lam{t} \;\mid\; \App{t}{u} \;\mid\; \tRef{h}
  \qquad (i \in \mathbb{N},\ h \in \Hash).
\]
A $\lambda$ binds without a name; $\Var{i}$ refers to the $i$-th enclosing binder, counting from
$0$ innermost. $\tRef{h}$ names another stored definition \emph{by hash}, never by identifier.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Well-scopedness and closedness]\label{def:closed}
\src{ast.re: max\_free\_index, is\_closed} Write $\vdash_n t$ (``$t$ is scoped under $n$
binders'') for the least relation with
\[
  \inferrule{i < n}{\vdash_n \Var{i}}\qquad
  \inferrule{\vdash_{n+1} t}{\vdash_n \Lam{t}}\qquad
  \inferrule{\vdash_n t \\ \vdash_n u}{\vdash_n \App{t}{u}}\qquad
  \inferrule{\ }{\vdash_n \tRef{h}}.
\]
$t$ is \emph{closed}, written $\isclosed{t}$, iff $\vdash_0 t$. Note $\tRef{h}$ is closed for
every $h$: a reference carries no free variable, which is why unfolding it later needs no index
adjustment.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Shift, substitution, contraction]\label{def:beta}
\src{ast.re: shift, subst, beta} The TAPL Chapter~6 operations, extended so that $\tRef{h}$ is
inert (it is closed, so no index crosses it):
\begin{align*}
  \shift{d}{c}{\Var{k}} &=
    \begin{cases} \Var{k} & k < c\\ \Var{k+d} & k \ge c\end{cases}
  & \shift{d}{c}{\Lam{t}} &= \Lam{\shift{d}{c+1}{t}}\\
  \shift{d}{c}{\App{t}{u}} &= \App{\shift{d}{c}{t}}{\shift{d}{c}{u}}
  & \shift{d}{c}{\tRef{h}} &= \tRef{h}\\[4pt]
  \subst{j}{s}{\Var{k}} &=
    \begin{cases} s & k = j\\ \Var{k} & k \ne j\end{cases}
  & \subst{j}{s}{\Lam{t}} &= \Lam{\subst{j+1}{\shift{1}{0}{s}}{t}}\\
  \subst{j}{s}{\App{t}{u}} &= \App{\subst{j}{s}{t}}{\subst{j}{s}{u}}
  & \subst{j}{s}{\tRef{h}} &= \tRef{h}.
\end{align*}
Beta-contraction of $\App{(\Lam{t})}{v}$ is, in the TAPL convention,
\[
  \mathsf{beta}(t, v) \;\defeq\; \shift{-1}{0}{\;\subst{0}{\shift{1}{0}{v}}{t}\;}.
\]
\end{definition}

\subsection{Surface terms and $\alpha$-canonicity}

\begin{definition}[Surface terms]\label{def:surface}
\src{surface\_ast.re} Surface terms carry names, with a \emph{single} identifier leaf:
\[
  s \;::=\; \sVar{x} \;\mid\; \sLam{x}{s} \;\mid\; \App{s}{s'}
  \qquad (x \in \Name).
\]
An identifier may be a use of an enclosing $\lambda$-bound name or a free name --- the parser
cannot tell which; resolution decides, innermost binder first (\cref{def:elab}). Names occur
\emph{only} in surface terms.
\end{definition}

The core syntax \emph{is} the $\alpha$-canonical form: de Bruijn indices identify
$\alpha$-equivalent terms definitionally, so arbor's per-language canonicalizer is the identity
\src{canonicalize.re}. We record this as the property that discharges the substrate's minimum
canonicalization requirement (\texttt{03}: ``every language should collapse $\alpha$-equivalence
so that $\lambda x.x$ and $\lambda y.y$ hash identically'').

\begin{example}[$\alpha$-collapse]\label{ex:alpha}
$\lambda x.\,x$ and $\lambda y.\,y$ both denote the single core term $\Lam{\Var{0}}$;
$\lambda x.\lambda y.\,x$ and $\lambda a.\lambda b.\,a$ both denote $\Lam{\Lam{\Var{1}}}$. In the
\texttt{p4} REPL, \texttt{const}, \texttt{konst}, and \texttt{tru} all resolve to one hash.
\end{example}

\section{Part II --- The term store}\label{sec:store}

The store is one level of arbor's four-layer architecture (\texttt{06-architecture}): it holds
content-addressed definitions and nothing about names. Definitions are stored \emph{shallowly}
--- one node at a time, children referenced by hash --- and the deep term is recovered by
walking the store.

\subsection{Nodes, hashing, and the store}

\begin{definition}[Shallow nodes]\label{def:node}
\src{node.re, +\,Ref} A node is one constructor deep, with hash-typed children:
\[
  n \;::=\; \nVar{i} \;\mid\; \nLam{h} \;\mid\; \nApp{h_1}{h_2} \;\mid\; \nRef{h}
  \qquad (i \in \mathbb{N},\ h,h_1,h_2 \in \Hash).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Hash]\label{def:hash}
\src{node.re, hash.re} $\Hash$ is an opaque type with an \emph{injective} map
$\hashof{\cdot} : \Node \to \Hash$. Injectivity idealizes cryptographic collision-resistance:
\[
  \hashof{n_1} = \hashof{n_2} \;\Longrightarrow\; n_1 = n_2. \tag{$\star$}
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[The concrete hash is one realization]\label{rem:concrete-hash}
Prototype \texttt{p4} realizes $\hashof{\cdot}$ as $\mathrm{BLAKE2B}$ over a tag-byte encoding:
$\nVar{k}\mapsto \mathtt{0x01}\Vert\mathrm{be}_{64}(k)$,
$\nLam{h}\mapsto \mathtt{0x02}\Vert h$,
$\nApp{h_1}{h_2}\mapsto \mathtt{0x03}\Vert h_1\Vert h_2$
(and, here, $\nRef{h}\mapsto \mathtt{0x05}\Vert h$). Determinism plus collision-resistance is
exactly what ($\star$) abstracts; no proof below inspects the encoding.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Store]\label{def:store}
\src{store.re} A term store is a finite partial function $\Store : \Hash \pfn \Node$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Ingest and reconstruct]\label{def:ingest}
\src{store.re: ingest, register\_node, reconstruct} $\ingest$ registers a deep term
bottom-up, keying each node by its hash (content-addressing makes this hash-consing:
re-registering an existing node is a no-op):
\begin{align*}
  \ingest(\Store, \Var{i}) &= (\Store', \hashof{\nVar{i}}),\ \Store'=\upd{\Store}{\hashof{\nVar{i}}}{\nVar{i}}\\
  \ingest(\Store, \Lam{t}) &= \text{let } (\Store_1,h)=\ingest(\Store,t) \text{ in } \bigl(\upd{\Store_1}{\hashof{\nLam{h}}}{\nLam{h}},\ \hashof{\nLam{h}}\bigr)\\
  \ingest(\Store, \App{t}{u}) &= \text{ingest both, then register } \nApp{h_t}{h_u}\\
  \ingest(\Store, \tRef{h}) &= \bigl(\upd{\Store}{\hashof{\nRef{h}}}{\nRef{h}},\ \hashof{\nRef{h}}\bigr).
\end{align*}
Crucially, ingesting $\tRef{h}$ does \emph{not} ingest $h$: the referent is an already-stored
definition, cited by hash. $\reconstruct : \Hash \pfn \Term$ walks back, stopping at leaves:
$\reconstruct(\Store,h)$ follows $\Store(h)$, recursing into $\nLam{}/\nApp{}$ children and
returning $\Var{i}$ / $\tRef{h'}$ unchanged; it is undefined on $h \notin \dom(\Store)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[No definition sort]\label{rem:nosort}
The store carries no marker distinguishing ``definitions'' from shared structure: storage is
shallow, so $\dom(\Store)$ contains every subterm of everything ingested, and hash-equality is
the only identity there is. Definition-hood is conferred by \emph{naming} (\textsc{Bind},
\cref{def:transitions}). A store-level definition sort is mint-flavored (\texttt{p11}'s
\texttt{Named} wrapper carries one) and is deferred with the mint axis; consequently migration
is a \emph{whole-store} rewrite (\cref{def:cascade}).
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[References]\label{def:refs}
For a term $t$, $\refs(t) \defeq \{\, h \mid \tRef{h} \text{ occurs in } t\,\}$; for a stored
hash, $\refs(\Store,h) \defeq \refs(\reconstruct(\Store,h))$. Since $\reconstruct$ stops at
$\tRef{}$ leaves, $\refs$ collects a definition's \emph{direct} citations, not their transitive
closure.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Well-formedness]\label{def:wf}
\src{03-content-addressing.md: ``hashes never dangle''} Write $\closedin{\Store}{h}$ for
``$h \in \dom(\Store)$ and $\isclosed{\reconstruct(\Store,h)}$''. $\Store$ is
\emph{well-formed}, $\wf(\Store)$, iff:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\roman*)]
  \item every $h \in \dom(\Store)$ has $\reconstruct(\Store,h)$ defined (every structural child
  resolves --- no dangle);
  \item every $h \in \dom(\Store)$ has $\closedin{\Store}{h'}$ for each
  $h' \in \refs(\Store,h)$ (reference targets denote closed terms --- the load-bearing
  \texttt{p4} invariant that lets \textsc{E-Ref} unfold with no shifting);
  \item the \emph{reference graph}
  $\{\,(h,h') \mid h \in \dom(\Store),\ h' \in \refs(\Store,h)\,\}$ is acyclic.
\end{enumerate}
Clause (ii) is not circular: whether $h'$ is closed depends only on
$\reconstruct(\Store,h')$, which stops at $\tRef{}$ leaves --- a reference is closed regardless
of its own target.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Why acyclicity must be stated]\label{rem:acyclic}
Clause (iii) is not implied by the others: a store with $\Store(h) = \nRef{h}$ --- that is,
$\hashof{\nRef{h}} = h$ --- satisfies (i)--(ii), since $\reconstruct$ stops at $\tRef{}$
leaves and a reference leaf is closed no matter its target. Injectivity ($\star$) alone is
consistent with such a fixpoint; a concrete hash function merely makes it unforgeable. Clause
(iii) is what makes the combined structural-and-reference graph well-founded, so that
migration's rewrite is definable by recursion (\cref{def:cascade}); evaluation does not need it
(\textsc{E-Ref} on a cyclic reference simply diverges).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Evaluation}

Evaluation is call-by-value to weak head normal form (no reduction under $\lambda$), reading
$\Store$ only to unfold references --- Cardelli's linking semantics
\cite{Cardelli1997Linking}. It \textbf{takes no namespace argument}; this is where
$\Names$-independence becomes syntactic.

\begin{definition}[Values]\label{def:value}
For closed terms the only weak-head values are abstractions: $\val ::= \Lam{t}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Evaluation relation]\label{def:eval}
\src{eval.re} $\evals{\Store}{t}{\val}$ is the least relation closed under
\begin{mathpar}
  \inferrule*[right=E-Lam]{\ }{\evals{\Store}{\Lam{t}}{\Lam{t}}}
  \and
  \inferrule*[right=E-Ref]{h \in \dom(\Store) \\ \evals{\Store}{\reconstruct(\Store,h)}{\val}}
            {\evals{\Store}{\tRef{h}}{\val}}
  \\
  \inferrule*[right=E-App]
    {\evals{\Store}{t}{\Lam{t_0}} \\ \evals{\Store}{u}{\val_u} \\
     \evals{\Store}{\mathsf{beta}(t_0,\val_u)}{\val}}
    {\evals{\Store}{\App{t}{u}}{\val}}
\end{mathpar}
There is no rule for a head $\Var{i}$: on closed terms it never occurs at an evaluation position
(\cref{lem:closed-no-stuck}); \texttt{p4} classifies it \textsf{Stuck} only to keep its function
total. Divergence is the absence of any derivation (e.g.\ $\Omega$, \cref{ex:omega}).
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Determinism]\label{lem:determinism}
If $\evals{\Store}{t}{\val_1}$ and $\evals{\Store}{t}{\val_2}$ then $\val_1 = \val_2$.
Consequently $\Downarrow$ induces a partial function $\mathsf{eval} : \Store \times \Term \pfn
\Val$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The rules are syntax-directed: the shape of $t$ (and, for \textsc{E-Ref}, $\Store(h)$) selects
at most one rule. The premises are \emph{not} structurally smaller (\textsc{E-App}'s contractum,
\textsc{E-Ref}'s unfolding), so induct on the first \emph{derivation}, inverting the second at
each step. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Closed terms never get stuck at the head]\label{lem:closed-no-stuck}
Suppose $\wf(\Store)$, $\isclosed{t}$, and --- the premise \cref{rem:nostuck-gap} shows to be
indispensable --- every $h \in \refs(t)$ has $\closedin{\Store}{h}$. Then no evaluation
position reached from $t$ has a free $\Var{}$ at its head; hence the missing $\Var$-rule loses
no closed-term behavior. Concretely, if $\evals{\Store}{t}{\val}$ then $\val$ is closed
\emph{and} every $h \in \refs(\val)$ has $\closedin{\Store}{h}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
$\mathsf{beta}$ preserves closedness (\cref{def:closed}, \cref{def:beta}), and so does
reference unfolding: the unfolded target is closed by the new premise, and \emph{its}
references are closed by $\wf$ (ii) --- which is the only place clause (ii) is used. A closed
weak-head normal form has an abstraction at its head. The reference clause has to travel in
the conclusion as well as the hypothesis, because the \textsc{E-App} case contracts against a
value obtained from the function position and needs that value's references. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why the reference premise cannot be dropped]\label{rem:nostuck-gap}
Stated with only $\wf(\Store)$ and $\isclosed{t}$ --- as this lemma was in the first version of
this paper --- the claim is \emph{false}, and the Agda development refutes it
(\texttt{Arbor/Core/Counterexamples.agda}). The gap: $\isclosed{\tRef{h}}$ holds for
\emph{every} $h$, since a reference carries no free variable (\cref{def:closed}), while $\wf$
constrains only the references \emph{of stored entries}. Nothing forces a stored hash to
reconstruct to a closed term, and nothing can: storage is shallow, so $\dom(\Store)$ is full of
open subterm nodes by design (\cref{rem:nosort}).

For a counterexample take $\Store = \{\,\hashof{\nVar 5} \mapsto \nVar 5,\;
\hashof{\nLam{\hashof{\nVar 5}}} \mapsto \nLam{\hashof{\nVar 5}}\,\}$. Nothing in it has a
reference, so clauses (ii) and (iii) are vacuous, and both entries reconstruct, so (i) holds:
$\wf(\Store)$. Yet with $h = \hashof{\nLam{\hashof{\nVar 5}}}$ we have $\isclosed{\tRef{h}}$
and $\evals{\Store}{\tRef{h}}{\Lam{\Var 5}}$, whose value is open.

The repair is the premise above. It is not a new burden: it is exactly \textsc{Ingest}'s second
premise and coherence clause (b), so it is available at every point the lemma is invoked ---
once \textsc{Eval} carries $\closedin{\Store}{h}$, which is why \cref{def:transitions} states
it (\cref{rem:eval-premise}).
\end{remark}

\begin{example}[Divergence]\label{ex:omega}
Let $\delta = \Lam{\App{\Var 0}{\Var 0}}$ and $\Omega = \App{\delta}{\delta}$, both closed.
$\Omega$ has no $\Downarrow$-derivation: \textsc{E-App} reduces $\Omega$ to $\Omega$. It is the
canonical non-cacheable term (\cref{sec:cache}).
\end{example}

\section{Part III --- The namespace}\label{sec:naming}

Names are an editing-layer concern (\texttt{04-naming-layer}). The namespace maps names to
hashes and is queried at edit time; stored programs carry only $\tRef{}$.

\begin{definition}[Namespace]\label{def:ns}
\src{namespace.re} A namespace is a finite partial function $\Names : \Name \pfn \Hash$, with
$\Name$ an opaque string type. It is functional in the name (a name resolves to at most one
hash) but not injective (aliases --- several names for one hash --- are free).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Resolution]\label{def:resolve}
\src{namespace.re: resolve} $\resolves{\Names}{x}{h}$ iff $\Names(x) = h$. There are exactly
two outcomes --- found, or not found ($x \notin \dom(\Names)$); suffix-based,
possibly-ambiguous resolution is an editing-layer elaboration over this primitive, deferred.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Elaboration: surface $\to$ core]\label{def:elab}
\src{resolver.re} Under a context $\Gamma$ (a list of bound names, innermost first),
elaboration $\Gamma;\Names \vdash s \Rrightarrow t$ replaces each identifier by its de Bruijn
index when $\lambda$-bound --- innermost binder wins --- and by a reference when free:
\begin{mathpar}
  \inferrule*[right=El-Bound]{i \text{ least with } \Gamma(i) = x}
            {\Gamma;\Names \vdash \sVar{x} \Rrightarrow \Var{i}}
  \and
  \inferrule*[right=El-Free]{x \notin \Gamma \\ \resolves{\Names}{x}{h}}
            {\Gamma;\Names \vdash \sVar{x} \Rrightarrow \tRef{h}}
  \\
  \inferrule*[right=El-Lam]{x{:}\Gamma;\Names \vdash s \Rrightarrow t}
            {\Gamma;\Names \vdash \sLam{x}{s} \Rrightarrow \Lam{t}}
  \and
  \inferrule*[right=El-App]{\Gamma;\Names \vdash s \Rrightarrow t \\ \Gamma;\Names \vdash s' \Rrightarrow t'}
            {\Gamma;\Names \vdash \App{s}{s'} \Rrightarrow \App{t}{t'}}
\end{mathpar}
The least-index premise makes shadowing unambiguous ($\lambda x.\lambda x.\,x$ elaborates only
to $\Lam{\Lam{\Var{0}}}$), and the two identifier rules are disjoint, so elaboration is a
partial \emph{function}. It fails (``unbound name'') when an identifier is neither
$\lambda$-bound nor in the namespace. The result is name-free by construction, giving the
substrate invariant as a typing fact, not a runtime assertion:
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Names never enter the store]\label{prop:namefree}
If $\Gamma;\Names \vdash s \Rrightarrow t$ then $t$ is a core term (\cref{def:core}), which has
no name leaf. Hence every ingested definition is name-free.
\end{proposition}

\begin{lemma}[Elaboration discharges the ingest premises]\label{lem:elab-premises}
If $\Gamma;\Names \vdash s \Rrightarrow t$ then $\vdash_{|\Gamma|} t$ and
$\refs(t) \subseteq \ran(\Names)$. In particular a top-level elaboration ($\Gamma$ empty)
produces a \emph{closed} term all of whose references lie in $\ran(\Names)$ --- under a coherent
configuration (\cref{def:config}) these are exactly the premises of the \textsc{Ingest}
transition (\cref{def:transitions}).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Induction on the elaboration derivation: \textsc{El-Bound} yields $\Var{i}$ with $i < |\Gamma|$;
\textsc{El-Free} yields $\tRef{h}$ with $h = \Names(x) \in \ran(\Names)$; \textsc{El-Lam}
extends $\Gamma$ by one. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Printing: core $\to$ surface]\label{def:print}
\src{pretty.re} Printing renders a core term back to surface syntax, choosing names as it goes.
Under a context $\Delta$ (the printed names of enclosing binders, innermost first), the
judgment $\Delta;\Names \vdash t \Lleftarrow s$ (``$t$ prints as $s$'') is the least relation
closed under
\begin{mathpar}
  \inferrule*[right=P-Var]{i \text{ least with } \Delta(i) = x}
            {\Delta;\Names \vdash \Var{i} \Lleftarrow \sVar{x}}
  \and
  \inferrule*[right=P-Ref]{x \notin \Delta \\ \resolves{\Names}{x}{h}}
            {\Delta;\Names \vdash \tRef{h} \Lleftarrow \sVar{x}}
  \\
  \inferrule*[right=P-Lam]{x{:}\Delta;\Names \vdash t \Lleftarrow s}
            {\Delta;\Names \vdash \Lam{t} \Lleftarrow \sLam{x}{s}}
  \and
  \inferrule*[right=P-App]{\Delta;\Names \vdash t \Lleftarrow s \\ \Delta;\Names \vdash t' \Lleftarrow s'}
            {\Delta;\Names \vdash \App{t}{t'} \Lleftarrow \App{s}{s'}}
\end{mathpar}
For a stored hash, printing $h$ means printing $\reconstruct(\Store,h)$ under
$\Delta = \emptyset$; the top-level hash is never itself replaced by a name. The judgment is
deliberately a \emph{relation} --- any binder name at \textsc{P-Lam}, any alias at
\textsc{P-Ref} --- whose leaf side conditions silently reject capturing choices;
\texttt{pretty.re} is one deterministic refinement (fresh-name supply, alphabetically-first
alias). An unnamed-but-historical reference renders as the orphan form $x(\mathsf{v}N)$
(\cref{def:orphan}), a display form outside the surface grammar; printing is undefined at a
hash with no name and no history.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Printing inverts to elaboration]\label{prop:print-elab}
If $\Delta;\Names \vdash t \Lleftarrow s$ then $\Delta;\Names \vdash s \Rrightarrow t$:
whatever rendering the printer chooses, re-resolving it recovers exactly the stored term.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Induction on the print derivation; each \textsc{P} rule's side condition is precisely the
matching \textsc{El} rule's premise (\textsc{P-Var}'s least index is \textsc{El-Bound}'s;
\textsc{P-Ref}'s $x \notin \Delta$ selects \textsc{El-Free}). \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Elaboration round-trip, up to name choice]\label{prop:elab-print}
Say $s \approx_{\Gamma,\Names} s'$ iff $s$ and $s'$ elaborate under $\Gamma;\Names$ to the same
core term. If $\Gamma;\Names \vdash s \Rrightarrow t$ and every reference in $t$ has a name
outside $\Gamma$, then some $s'$ has $\Gamma;\Names \vdash t \Lleftarrow s'$, and every such
$s'$ satisfies $s \approx_{\Gamma,\Names} s'$. Concretely, $\approx$ is generated by exactly
two moves: capture-avoiding renaming of a $\lambda$-binder, and swapping a free identifier for
an alias ($\sVar{x} \leftrightarrow \sVar{y}$ when $\Names(x) = \Names(y)$).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Existence: induction on $t$, choosing binder names away from the finitely many aliases the
subterm's references need. Membership in $\approx$ is immediate from \cref{prop:print-elab}
plus elaboration being a function (\cref{def:elab}). The two-move characterization is by
induction on a pair of $\approx$-related terms: equal elaborations force equal shapes, and at
an identifier leaf the only freedom is which name selected the same index (binder renaming) or
the same hash (alias swap). \qed
\end{proof}

\section{Part IV --- The evaluation cache}\label{sec:cache}

Arbor's Attachment layer holds two kinds of data keyed to definitions: \emph{asserted} entries
(human-authored, mutable --- the namespace) and \emph{derived} entries (computed by a known
procedure, immutable for that procedure --- here, evaluation results). Modeling the cache is how
we make ``already-evaluated terms are never re-evaluated'' a theorem rather than a hope.

\begin{definition}[Evaluation cache]\label{def:cache}
\src{attachment.re, eval.re} The cache is a finite partial function
$\Cache : \Hash \pfn \Hash$, a \emph{derived} aspect mapping the hash of a term to the hash of
its value, \emph{itself ingested into the store}. Both key and value are store terms; the
cache never touches surface syntax. Its discipline: an entry may be written only for a
\emph{defined} evaluation, and once written is never overwritten.
\begin{mathpar}
  \inferrule*[right=Cache-sound]
    {h \in \dom(\Cache)}
    {\evals{\Store}{\tRef{h}}{\reconstruct(\Store,\Cache(h))}}
\end{mathpar}
In particular $\Cache(h) \in \dom(\Store)$, and --- values being closed abstractions ---
$\closedin{\Store}{\Cache(h)}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Why \textsf{StepLimit} is not cached]\label{rem:nostepcache}
\texttt{p4} evaluates under a step budget and returns \textsf{Value}, \textsf{Stuck}, or
\textsf{StepLimit}, and it deliberately never caches \textsf{StepLimit} \src{eval.re}. In our
terms \textsf{StepLimit} is ``no derivation \emph{yet}'': it is not a defined result, so
\textsc{Cache-sound} forbids storing it. Caching it would be unsound because a larger budget can
turn it into a value; only defined ($\Downarrow$) results are stable
(\cref{thm:stability},~\cref{cor:fuel}).
\end{remark}

\section{Part V --- The edit calculus}\label{sec:edit}

Edits are transitions on a configuration. Binding history is append-only, so we carry it
explicitly.

\begin{definition}[Binding history]\label{def:history}
\src{namespace.re: history\_event, append\_history} $\Hist : \Name \pfn (\;(\Hash \cup
\{\bot\}) \times \Time\;)^{*}$, newest event first. $(\mathsf{Some}\,h,\tau)$ is a bind/rebind to
$h$ at time $\tau$; $(\bot,\tau)$ is an unbind tombstone. The current binding of $x$ is the head
if it is $\mathsf{Some}$, else $x$ is unbound.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Configuration and coherence]\label{def:config}
$C = \Config{\Store}{\Names}{\Cache}{\Hist}$. $C$ is \emph{coherent} iff:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\alph*)]
  \item $\wf(\Store)$ (\cref{def:wf});
  \item $\closedin{\Store}{h}$ for every $h \in \ran(\Names)$ (a name may assert
  definition-hood only of a closed stored term, \cref{rem:nosort});
  \item $\closedin{\Store}{h}$ for every hash $h$ in a $\mathsf{Some}$ event of $\Hist$
  (orphans remain closed, reconstructible terms --- what makes \cref{def:orphan}'s rendering
  meaningful);
  \item history coherence (\cref{thm:histcoh}) and \textsc{Cache-sound} (\cref{def:cache}).
\end{enumerate}
Fresh timestamps $\tau$ are drawn monotonically; we leave the clock abstract. Every transition
below preserves coherence (\cref{thm:wf}), so all configurations reachable from the empty one
are coherent.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Structural and naming edits}

\begin{definition}[Core transitions]\label{def:transitions}
\begin{mathpar}
  \inferrule*[right=Ingest]
    {\isclosed{t} \\ \forall h' \in \refs(t).\ \closedin{\Store}{h'} \\ \ingest(\Store,t) = (\Store',\_)}
    {\Config{\Store}{\Names}{\Cache}{\Hist} \steps \Config{\Store'}{\Names}{\Cache}{\Hist}}
  \and
  \inferrule*[right=Eval]{\closedin{\Store}{h} \\ \evals{\Store}{\tRef{h}}{\val} \\
                          h \notin \dom(\Cache) \\
                          \ingest(\Store,\val) = (\Store', h_{\val})}
    {\Config{\Store}{\Names}{\Cache}{\Hist} \steps
     \Config{\Store'}{\Names}{\upd{\Cache}{h}{h_{\val}}}{\Hist}}
  \\
  \inferrule*[right=Bind]{x \notin \dom(\Names) \\ \closedin{\Store}{h}}
    {\Config{\Store}{\Names}{\Cache}{\Hist} \steps
     \Config{\Store}{\upd{\Names}{x}{h}}{\Cache}{\upd{\Hist}{x}{(\mathsf{Some}\,h,\tau){:}\Hist(x)}}}
  \and
  \inferrule*[right=Rebind]{\closedin{\Store}{h}}
    {\Config{\Store}{\Names}{\Cache}{\Hist} \steps
     \Config{\Store}{\upd{\Names}{x}{h}}{\Cache}{\upd{\Hist}{x}{(\mathsf{Some}\,h,\tau){:}\Hist(x)}}}
  \\
  \inferrule*[right=Unbind]{x \in \dom(\Names)}
    {\Config{\Store}{\Names}{\Cache}{\Hist} \steps
     \Config{\Store}{\Names\setminus x}{\Cache}{\upd{\Hist}{x}{(\bot,\tau){:}\Hist(x)}}}
\end{mathpar}
$\bind$, $\rebind$, $\unbind$ touch only $\Names$ and $\Hist$; $\Store$ is untouched. Renaming
is derived as $\unbind$ then $\bind$ \src{namespace.re: rename}. The premises are load-bearing:
\textsc{Ingest}'s are established by elaboration (\cref{lem:elab-premises}) but must be stated
here or preservation (\cref{thm:wf}) fails; \textsc{Bind}/\textsc{Rebind} require
$\closedin{\Store}{h}$ and nothing more --- \emph{any} closed stored hash may be named
(\cref{rem:nosort}). \textsc{Rebind} also binds fresh names, as in \texttt{p11};
\textsc{Bind} is its first-bind special case.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[\textsc{Eval}'s closedness premise]\label{rem:eval-premise}
$\closedin{\Store}{h}$ on \textsc{Eval} was absent from the first version of this paper, and its
absence broke \cref{thm:wf}: that proof's \textsc{Eval} case appeals to
\cref{lem:closed-no-stuck} to know the ingested value is closed, and the lemma needs $h$'s
target to be closed (\cref{rem:nostuck-gap}) --- which no other premise of the rule supplies.
It costs nothing operationally: \texttt{p4} evaluates \emph{definitions}, and a named hash is
closed by coherence clause (b), so every \textsc{Eval} an editor would perform already
satisfies it. Found while mechanizing; see \texttt{formalism/decisions.md}.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Orphans]\label{def:orphan}
\src{namespace.re: reverse\_lookup\_with\_version} A hash $h$ is an \emph{orphan} if it appears
in some $\Hist(x)$ but is not the current binding of any name. Its version is the $1$-indexed
position of the \emph{first} occurrence of $h$ among the $\mathsf{Some}$ events of $\Hist(x)$,
oldest first (a hash rebound later re-renders as its first version); the printer renders it
$x(\mathsf{v}N)$.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Migration}

Migration edits a definition and propagates the change through the store under a chosen scope.
Without mints there is no unit of intent to scope by (\cref{rem:nosort}): propagation is a
\emph{whole-store} structural rewrite, and names simply follow their hashes.

\begin{definition}[Callers]\label{def:callers}
\src{store.re: callers\_of, callers\_closure}
$\callers(\Store,h) \defeq \{\, g \in \dom(\Store) \mid h \in \refs(\Store,g)\,\}$;
$\callersstar(\Store,h)$ is its transitive closure. Callers are an \emph{observation}, not a
scoping device: migration below rewrites the whole store, and
$\callersstar(\Store,g_{\mathrm{old}})$, together with the structure above those entries, is
exactly the set it can change.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Reference substitution]\label{def:substrefs}
\src{follow\_clean.re: substitute\_in\_term} For a finite map $\rho : \Hash \pfn \Hash$,
$\substrefs{\rho}{t}$ rewrites every $\tRef{h}$ in $t$ with $h \in \dom(\rho)$ to $\tRef{\rho(h)}$,
leaving all else fixed.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Scope]\label{def:strategy}
\src{update\_strategy.re: strategy, filter} A strategy induces a \emph{propagation scope}
$P \subseteq \dom(\Store)$:
\[
  \Pin \mapsto \emptyset,\qquad
  \Follow \mapsto \dom(\Store),\qquad
  \Explicit(S) \mapsto S.
\]
These are three points in the design's two-axis space (\texttt{04}: \emph{scope} $\times$
\emph{user-in-loop}); \Pin{} is the empty-scope instance of the same machinery, not a special
case. The scope is consulted only where an edge \emph{crosses a reference} (\cref{def:cascade})
or a \emph{name binding} (\cref{def:migrate}); structure always propagates.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Migration rewrite]\label{def:cascade}
\src{update\_strategy.re: cascade\_pairs; follow\_clean.re} Fix a \emph{seed}
$g_{\mathrm{old}} \mapsto g_{\mathrm{new}}$ with $\closedin{\Store}{g_{\mathrm{old}}}$ and
$\closedin{\Store}{g_{\mathrm{new}}}$ (the new body was ingested beforehand), and a scope $P$
(\cref{def:strategy}). The rewrite $\rho : \dom(\Store) \to \Hash$ is defined by recursion:
\[
  \rho(g_{\mathrm{old}}) = g_{\mathrm{new}}, \qquad\text{otherwise}\quad
  \rho(h) = \hashof{\rho^{\#}(\Store(h))},
\]
\[
  \rho^{\#}(\nVar{i}) = \nVar{i}, \quad
  \rho^{\#}(\nLam{c}) = \nLam{\rho(c)}, \quad
  \rho^{\#}(\nApp{c_1}{c_2}) = \nApp{\rho(c_1)}{\rho(c_2)}, \quad
  \rho^{\#}(\nRef{r}) = \nRef{\hat\rho(r)},
\]
where $\hat\rho(r) = \rho(r)$ if $r \in P \cup \{g_{\mathrm{old}}\}$ and $\hat\rho(r) = r$
otherwise: structural edges always propagate, reference edges only into scope. The recursion is
well-founded: structural edges terminate by $\wf$ (i), reference edges by $\wf$ (iii), and a
mixed cycle projects (via $\reconstruct$, which folds structure into $\refs$) onto a
reference-graph cycle, contradicting (iii). Every node in the image of $\rho^{\#}$ is
registered; $\Store'$ is $\Store$ extended by them. An entry \emph{follows} iff
$\rho(h) \neq h$ --- exactly the entries reaching $g_{\mathrm{old}}$ through structure and
scope-admitted references; everything else re-hashes to itself.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Dependency order]\label{def:deporder}
For finite $K \subseteq \dom(\Store)$, a \emph{dependency order} on $K$ is a linear order
$g_1, \dots, g_k$ enumerating $K$ such that whenever $g_j$ transitively references $g_i$ (a
path $g_j \to \cdots \to g_i$ in the reference graph) with $g_i, g_j \in K$, then $i < j$ ---
referents before referrers. By $\wf$ clause (iii) a dependency order always exists (any
topological sort of the acyclic reference graph restricted to $K$); without (iii) it need not
(\cref{rem:acyclic}).
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Sequential passes compute $\rho$]\label{lem:cascade-order}
Let $K = \{h \mid \rho(h) \neq h\}$. Processing $K$ in \emph{any} dependency order
(\cref{def:deporder}) --- rewriting each entry's reconstruction with the substitution
accumulated so far (\cref{def:substrefs}, seeded with
$g_{\mathrm{old}} \mapsto g_{\mathrm{new}}$) and re-ingesting --- computes exactly $\rho$ on
$K$, independent of the order chosen. This is the bridge to \texttt{p11}'s implementation,
whose \texttt{cascade\_pairs} is such a pass (on its ordering, see \texttt{decisions.md}).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Well-founded induction along the reference order: when $g$ is processed, every in-scope
referent $r$ with $\rho(r) \neq r$ has already been processed, so the accumulated substitution
agrees with $\hat\rho$ on $\refs(\Store,g)$; re-ingesting the rewritten reconstruction then
registers node-for-node the $\rho^{\#}$-image of $g$'s structure. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The untyped gate is total]\label{rem:gate}
\texttt{p11} gates its cascade by re-typechecking each rewritten caller
\src{update\_strategy.re: Typecheck.check\_top}. The untyped analogue --- rewritten entries
closed, references denoting closed stored terms --- holds \emph{by construction}: $\rho^{\#}$
preserves node shapes, rewriting preserves closedness, and referents are registered before
referrers. So untyped migration cannot fail: $\Follow$ always succeeds, and the abort path of
\cref{def:migrate} is vacuous here. The atomicity contract is stated anyway --- it is what the
typed successor's gate needs, and \emph{all} interesting failure modes of $\Follow$ arrive
with types.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Atomic multi-rebind and the migration transition]\label{def:migrate}
\src{namespace.re: multi\_rebind; update\_strategy.re: apply\_with\_named} Let $\rho$ be the
migration rewrite for $(g_{\mathrm{old}} \mapsto g_{\mathrm{new}}, P)$, $\Store'$ its extended
store, and $x_0$ the edited name ($\Names(x_0) = g_{\mathrm{old}}$). Names follow their
hashes, guarded by the same scope:
\[
  U \;=\; \{(x_0, g_{\mathrm{new}})\} \;\cup\;
  \{\,(y, \rho(\Names(y))) \mid y \in \dom(\Names),\ \Names(y) \in P,\
     \rho(\Names(y)) \neq \Names(y)\,\}.
\]
Under \Follow{} every name whose meaning changed rebinds, aliases of $g_{\mathrm{old}}$
included; under \Pin{} only $x_0$. $\multirebind$ validates $U$ (every target
$\closedin{\Store'}{\cdot}$ --- automatic here, \cref{rem:gate}) and
\textbf{commits all-or-nothing}:
\begin{mathpar}
  \inferrule*[right=Migrate]
    {\Names(x_0) = g_{\mathrm{old}} \\ \closedin{\Store}{g_{\mathrm{new}}} \\
     \rho,\ \Store' \text{ as in \cref{def:cascade}} \\
     \multirebind(\Names,\Hist,U) = (\Names',\Hist')}
    {\Config{\Store}{\Names}{\Cache}{\Hist} \steps
     \Config{\Store'}{\Names'}{\Cache}{\Hist'}}
\end{mathpar}
On a validation failure, $\Names$ and $\Hist$ are untouched; $\Store$ may retain the candidate
hashes it accumulated (there is no orphan GC). Thus \textbf{only $\langle\Names,\Hist\rangle$
is transactional; $\Store$ is accumulate-only}.
\end{definition}

\section{Metatheory}\label{sec:meta}

We now state the guarantees. The first block (\crefrange{thm:alpha}{thm:wf}) concerns identity
and the store; the second (\crefrange{thm:stability}{thm:migosc}) is the evaluation and
migration payoff. Proof sketches indicate the eventual Agda obligations (\cref{sec:mech}).

\begin{theorem}[Hashing collapses exactly $\alpha$-equivalence]\label{thm:alpha}
For core terms $t,u$, ingesting yields the same hash iff $t = u$ (which, since core is de Bruijn,
is $\alpha$-equivalence of the surface terms they elaborate from). Formally, with $h_t,h_u$ the
roots of $\ingest(\Store,t),\ingest(\Store,u)$: $h_t = h_u \iff t = u$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
$(\Leftarrow)$ $\ingest$ is a function. $(\Rightarrow)$ induction on $t$ using injectivity
($\star$, \cref{def:hash}): equal root hashes force equal top nodes, hence equal child hashes,
hence (IH) equal subterms. \cref{ex:alpha} instantiates it. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[$\alpha$-equivalence, and not more]\label{rem:alpha-only}
The theorem is a biconditional on \emph{terms}, so it says as much by what it withholds. Equal
hashes force equal core terms --- not merely terms with equal meaning. In particular
$\tRef{h}$ and the term $h$ reconstructs to are \emph{different} objects with different hashes:
the reference node has its own encoding (\cref{def:node}), so how a definition is factored into
citations is part of its identity, and inlining a reference is not hash-preserving. That is a
real fork, and the opposite choice is available: Unison's reference node contributes the
\emph{referent's} hash to its parent, making inlining hash-preserving and $y = x$ with
$x = 1{+}1$ one object \src{Hashing/V2/Term.hs:135-146}. Both systems keep the indirection ---
without it there are no cross-definition edges and migration is vacuous (\cref{sec:scope}) ---
but they disagree about whether factoring is observable. Recorded, not yet argued, in
\texttt{formalism/open-questions.md}.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[No silent breakage]\label{thm:nsb}
If $C = \Config{\Store}{\Names}{\Cache}{\Hist} \steps \Config{\Store}{\Names'}{\Cache}{\Hist'}$
by \textsc{Bind}, \textsc{Rebind}, or \textsc{Unbind}, then $\Store$ is unchanged, and therefore
for every $h$, $\evals{\Store}{\tRef{h}}{\val}$ holds after the edit iff it held before. A name
edit changes only \emph{which} hash a name resolves to, never what any referenced hash computes.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The three rules (\cref{def:transitions}) modify only $\Names,\Hist$. $\Downarrow$
(\cref{def:eval}) mentions only $\Store$ and a term. So its extension is literally unchanged.
\qed
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[The \texttt{p3} pin test]\label{ex:nsb}
Let $\mathit{zero},\mathit{succ}$ be defined; $\mathit{one} = \App{\tRef{h_{\mathit{succ}}}}
{\tRef{h_{\mathit{zero}}}}$ at hash $h_1$; $\mathit{two} = \App{\tRef{h_{\mathit{succ}}}}
{\tRef{h_1}}$ at hash $h_2$. Rebinding the name $\mathit{one}$ to any hash leaves $\Store(h_2)$
--- and its child $\tRef{h_1}$ --- untouched, so $\mathit{two}$ still computes via the original
$\mathit{one}$. This is \cref{thm:nsb} in miniature.
\end{example}

\begin{theorem}[Monotonicity and immutability]\label{thm:mono}
Every transition has $\Store \subseteq \Store'$ (as partial functions), and no existing entry
$h \mapsto n$ is ever overwritten.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
\textsc{Bind}/\textsc{Rebind}/\textsc{Unbind} leave $\Store$ fixed. \textsc{Ingest},
\textsc{Eval} (which ingests the computed value), and the migration rewrite only add entries,
and each key is $\hashof{n}$ for the very node $n$ stored, so any collision on a key is with
an identical node ($\star$) --- a step that presupposes the store \emph{is} keyed by hash,
which no clause of \cref{def:wf} states and which the mechanization therefore carries as a
further coherence clause (\cref{sec:mech}). \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Coherence preservation]\label{thm:wf}
If $C$ is coherent (\cref{def:config}) and $C \steps C'$, then $C'$ is coherent.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Clause by clause. \emph{$\wf$ (i)--(ii):} \textsc{Ingest}'s premises supply closedness of the
new term's reference targets; its structural children are registered bottom-up, so
$\reconstruct$ is defined on every new entry. \textsc{Eval} ingests a value that is closed, and whose
references denote closed terms, by \cref{lem:closed-no-stuck} --- applicable exactly because
the rule now carries $\closedin{\Store}{h}$ (\cref{rem:eval-premise}). The
migration rewrite preserves both by construction (\cref{rem:gate}). Name edits do not touch
$\Store$.
\emph{$\wf$ (iii):} no existing node changes (\cref{thm:mono}), and every newly added node's
references point at pre-existing closed entries (\textsc{Ingest} premise, \textsc{Eval}'s
value) or at rewrites whose registration precedes their referrers' (\textsc{Migrate});
ordering new nodes by registration therefore extends any topological order of the old
reference graph, so no cycle is created.
\emph{(b), (c):} \textsc{Bind}/\textsc{Rebind} add bindings and $\mathsf{Some}$ events only
with $\closedin{\Store}{h}$; $\multirebind$'s targets are validated closed in $\Store'$;
\textsc{Unbind} only removes a binding and appends $\bot$; closedness of an existing entry is
stable because $\reconstruct$ at a stored hash never changes (\cref{thm:mono}). \emph{(d):}
\cref{thm:histcoh} and \cref{cor:cache}. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Evaluation stability under growth]\label{thm:stability}
Suppose $\wf(\Store)$, $\Store \subseteq \Store'$, and $\wf(\Store')$. Then for every $h$,
$\evals{\Store}{\tRef{h}}{\val}$ implies $\evals{\Store'}{\tRef{h}}{\val}$ (and, by
determinism, the values agree).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
$\reconstruct$ and $\Downarrow$ read only entries at hashes reachable from $h$ (through structure
and $\mathsf{ref}$-unfolding). All such entries lie in $\dom(\Store)$ and, by immutability
(\cref{thm:mono}), are identical in $\Store'$. Replay the derivation. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Cache soundness / no re-evaluation]\label{cor:cache}
If \textsc{Cache-sound} holds for $C$ and $C \steps C'$, it holds for $C'$: a written entry
stays valid in every future store. Hence a term is evaluated \emph{at most once} across the
whole history --- the cache never needs invalidation.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The only ways $\Store$ grows are \textsc{Ingest}/\textsc{Eval}/\textsc{Migrate}
(\cref{thm:mono}); apply \cref{thm:stability}, noting that
$\reconstruct(\cdot,\Cache(h))$ at a stored hash never changes under growth
(\cref{thm:mono}). \textsc{Eval} adds a cache entry that is sound by its premises (the value
it ingested reconstructs to itself); no rule removes or overwrites a cache entry. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Stability under transfer]\label{cor:transfer}
Let $\Store_0$ be a store fragment shared by two configurations, and $\Store$ any store with
$\Store_0 \subseteq \Store$. If $\evals{\Store_0}{\tRef{h}}{\val}$ then
$\evals{\Store}{\tRef{h}}{\val}$. In particular $\Store$ need not be a temporal successor of
$\Store_0$: it may be a \emph{different codebase} that happens to hold the same fragment, and
the two such codebases need not be comparable to each other.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
\cref{thm:stability}, unchanged --- which is the content. Nothing in that proof reads $\Store$'s
history; it replays a derivation over entries fixed by immutability, and immutability does not
care \emph{whose} store the entries sit in. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why transfer is worth separating from growth]\label{rem:transfer}
Stated only for growth, \cref{thm:stability} reads as a fact about one user's timeline. The same
proof gives the sharing property a commons needs: a derived result computed anywhere is valid
everywhere the inputs agree, so distributing evaluation results is sound without re-checking
them, and ``which of these definitions are known to pass their tests'' becomes a set
intersection rather than a computation. Unison relies on exactly this when it syncs test
results across codebases \src{docs/testing.markdown}. It is the form of the theorem the
computational-commons argument actually uses.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Migration is incremental]\label{cor:incremental}
After a \textsc{Migrate} step, every cache entry for a hash in the \emph{old} $\dom(\Store)$
remains valid; the set of terms that may require (re-)evaluation is contained in the
newly-registered hashes ($g_{\mathrm{new}}$ and the $\rho$-image).
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
$\Store \subseteq \Store'$; \cref{cor:cache} preserves old entries. New behavior lives only at
new hashes, which are exactly the ones added. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Only divergence is non-cacheable]\label{cor:fuel}
Model \texttt{p4}'s budget as $\mathsf{eval}_k$ with fuel $k$. Then $\mathsf{eval}_k(t)=\val
\Rightarrow \mathsf{eval}_{k+1}(t)=\val$ (fuel-monotonicity), and $\mathsf{eval}_k(t)=\val$ for
some $k$ iff $\evals{\Store}{t}{\val}$. So \textsf{StepLimit} (no $k$ succeeds \emph{yet}) is
precisely the non-stable outcome that \cref{rem:nostepcache} forbids caching, while
\textsf{Value}/\textsf{Stuck} are stable and cacheable.
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[History coherence]\label{thm:histcoh}
After any transition: $x \in \dom(\Names)$ with $\Names(x)=h$ iff the head of $\Hist(x)$ is
$(\mathsf{Some}\,h,\_)$; and $x \notin \dom(\Names)$ iff $\Hist(x)$ is empty or has a $\bot$
head.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Immediate for each of \textsc{Bind}/\textsc{Rebind}/\textsc{Unbind}, which prepend the matching
event; $\multirebind$ (\cref{def:migrate}) commits a batch of rebinds, each preserving the
invariant. Induction on transitions. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Migration atomicity and scope]\label{thm:migosc}
For a \textsc{Migrate} step with scope $P$:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\alph*)]
  \item \emph{(Atomicity)} $\langle\Names,\Hist\rangle$ advances iff $\multirebind$ validates;
  otherwise it is unchanged. (In this untyped instance validation cannot fail,
  \cref{rem:gate}; the contract is stated for the typed successor's gate.)
  \item \emph{(Scope)} the rewritten entries are exactly those with $\rho(h) \neq h$: the
  entries reaching $g_{\mathrm{old}}$ through structure and scope-admitted reference
  crossings. The rebound names are $x_0$ plus every name bound in scope whose hash changed.
  Under $\Pin$ nothing is rewritten and $g_{\mathrm{old}}$ becomes an orphan; under $\Follow$
  everything reaching $g_{\mathrm{old}}$ follows, names and aliases included; under
  $\Explicit(S)$, propagation crosses references (and name bindings) only into $S$. All of it
  is order-free by construction (\cref{def:cascade}); sequential implementations agree
  (\cref{lem:cascade-order}).
  \item \emph{(Preservation)} $\wf(\Store')$ and \cref{thm:nsb} still hold: entries that were
  \emph{not} rewritten keep their original reference edges, so their meaning is unchanged.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
(a) is the all-or-nothing contract of $\multirebind$ (\cref{def:migrate}). (b) unfolds
\cref{def:cascade,def:strategy,def:migrate}. (c) combines \cref{thm:wf} with the observation
that a non-rewritten entry's nodes are untouched (\cref{thm:mono}). \qed
\end{proof}

\section{Mechanization roadmap}\label{sec:mech}

The development lives in \texttt{formalism/agda/} (Agda 2.7.0, \texttt{agda-stdlib} 2.1) and is
checked with \texttt{agda -{}-safe}: no postulates, no termination pragmas, no unsolved metas.
Sixteen of this paper's eighteen numbered statements are discharged ---
\cref{thm:alpha,thm:nsb,thm:mono,thm:wf,thm:stability,thm:histcoh}, \cref{lem:determinism,lem:closed-no-stuck,lem:elab-premises},
\cref{prop:print-elab,prop:elab-print}, and \cref{cor:cache,cor:transfer,cor:fuel,cor:incremental};
\cref{prop:namefree} holds by construction. What remains is the construction of $\rho$ itself:
\cref{thm:migosc} is currently discharged from a \emph{specification} record that any rewrite
must satisfy, and \cref{lem:cascade-order} is not yet statable, since it quantifies over a
sequential pass that does not exist until $\rho$ is constructed. \texttt{make status} reports
this per label; \texttt{make labels} checks that no statement here lacks a counterpart there.

Four transcription choices are worth recording; the rest, with rationale, is in
\texttt{formalism/decisions.md} (2026-08-05) and \texttt{formalism/agda/README.md}.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Extrinsic scoping.} $\Term$ is not indexed by a scope level;
  \cref{def:closed} is transcribed as the inductive relation it already is, and closedness
  travels as an explicit proof. An intrinsic $\Term : \mathbb{N} \to \mathsf{Set}$ founders on
  shallow storage: $\dom(\Store)$ holds \emph{open} subterm nodes (\cref{rem:nosort}), so
  $\reconstruct$ would have to return a level existential, and the level is not canonical.
  $\alpha$-equivalence is definitional either way --- that is de Bruijn, not the indexing --- so
  \cref{thm:alpha}'s $(\Leftarrow)$ half is still a congruence. The price is that
  ``$\mathsf{beta}$ preserves closedness'' becomes an explicit lemma rather than a typing fact.
  \item \textbf{$\Hash$ as a record parameter, not a postulate.} The type, $\hashof{\cdot}$, and
  ($\star$) are fields of a record the development is parameterized over, so the paper's one
  axiom is an assumption discharged at instantiation rather than a hole in the logic. The record
  is parameterized over the \emph{entry functor} --- $\Node$ mentions $\Hash$, so sibling fields
  would be circular. $\Hash \defeq \Node$-as-a-free-type is exhibited as a consistency witness
  (it makes ($\star$) hold by constructor injectivity), which is what keeps the proved theorems
  from being vacuous; it remains rejected as the \emph{representation}, since interning collapses
  $\mathsf{ref}$.
  \item \textbf{The partial functions of this paper are relations.} $\reconstruct$ is an
  inductive judgment with a functionality lemma, as $\Downarrow$ and printing already are, and
  $\rho$ will be. As functions they would need termination arguments that do not hold of an
  arbitrary $\Store$ --- structural children may loop; that a stored hash reconstructs is
  precisely what $\wf$ (i) \emph{asserts}, so a definition may not assume it. The fueled
  $\mathsf{eval} : \mathbb{N} \to \Store \to \Term \to \mathsf{Result}$ is separate and total,
  and \cref{cor:fuel} relates the two.
  \item \textbf{Stores as functions into $\mathsf{Maybe}$}, keyed by decidable equality.
  Inclusion is then pointwise implication, which is how \cref{thm:mono,thm:stability} are
  stated, and \cref{thm:mono}'s two halves become one. Finiteness is not assumed at all yet; it
  enters when $\rho$ must enumerate $\dom(\Store)$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What mechanizing changed in this paper.} Four gaps, all now fixed above.
\cref{lem:closed-no-stuck} was \emph{false} as first stated and is refuted by a machine-checked
counterexample (\cref{rem:nostuck-gap}); repairing it forced a closedness premise onto
\textsc{Eval} (\cref{rem:eval-premise}), without which \cref{thm:wf} does not hold.
\cref{thm:mono}'s proof tacitly assumes the store is keyed by hash, which no clause of
\cref{def:wf} provides; the development carries it as an extra coherence clause.
\cref{thm:stability}'s two $\wf$ premises turn out unnecessary --- replaying the derivation needs
$\Store \subseteq \Store'$ and nothing more. And \cref{prop:elab-print}'s side condition is
redundant, since an elaboration derivation already witnesses it; that matters because with
$\Name$ abstract there is no fresh-name supply, so the sketch's ``choosing binder names'' cannot
be carried out --- but the printer can simply return the surface term it started from. Two
lemmas the paper used without stating are also now named: that re-reconstructing an ingested
term returns it, and that elaboration is a partial function. All are tracked in
\texttt{formalism/open-questions.md}.
